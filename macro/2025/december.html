Monthly Macro Highlights

Some of the caution seen in November began to appear in current activity. UK retail volumes fell again and China's property cash flows weakened, while US households continued to spend in selected categories.

December 2025Reporting window: 25 November–19 December 2025By Jiefu Zhang

Central question

Were weaker investment plans beginning to affect current spending and output?

There were clearer signs than in November. UK output and retail volumes both fell, while Chinese housing sales, new projects and developer funding weakened together. The United States was more mixed: total retail sales were flat, but online and restaurant spending still grew from a year earlier. The two rate cuts responded to the slowdown; they did not show that it had ended.

What changed this month

Earlier caution began to show up in current activity

US nominal retail sales: virtually flat

October receipts barely changed from September, although non-store retail and food services were still 9.0% and 4.1% higher than a year earlier.

UK retail volumes: −0.1% m/m

November sales fell after a 0.9% October decline; supermarket volumes recorded a fourth consecutive fall.

China November retail sales: +1.3% y/y

Growth slowed as property investment fell 15.9% and developer mortgage receipts fell 15.1% in January–November.

Two policy-rate cuts

The Federal Reserve lowered its target range to 3.5–3.75%, and the Bank of England cut Bank Rate to 3.75%.

Evidence and mechanism

The slowdown was clearer in some parts of the economy than others

November's survey evidence said businesses had become more careful about the next loan or project. December's releases showed where that care was reaching revenue. UK GDP fell 0.1% in October and over the latest three months, while November retail volumes fell after a much larger October decline. In China, weaker property sales reduced developer cash receipts at the same time that new starts and investment contracted. Those are connected outcomes: when financing and presales fall, developers commission less construction and purchase fewer materials; households exposed to housing wealth have less reason to increase discretionary spending.

The US evidence was less broad. November payrolls rose 64,000 and unemployment reached 4.6%, while October retail and food-service sales were virtually unchanged from September. Yet non-store retailers were 9.0% above a year earlier and restaurants 4.1% higher. A flat aggregate could therefore reflect timing and category substitution as well as lower total demand. The missing October household survey also meant the labour sequence was still not fully observed. [BLS, 16 December] [US Census, 16 December]

The Federal Reserve and Bank of England both cut rates in December. Overnight borrowing costs changed immediately, but many households and companies would not benefit until they refinanced. A firm also would not restart a cancelled project simply because rates fell by 25 basis points. The cuts showed that policymakers were concerned about the slowdown; they did not show that lower rates had already increased spending.

Answer

Earlier caution was beginning to affect current activity, especially in UK retail and Chinese housing. The US picture was less negative: hiring slowed and retail sales were flat, but online and restaurant spending still grew over the year. I did not yet see one common downturn across all three economies. The next question was whether the weak areas would continue after the two rate cuts.

Equities and credit improved while the latest activity data weakened. That is consistent with investors valuing lower discount rates and a policy backstop before expecting stronger near-term revenue. Falling Brent also helped inflation expectations, but it carried two possible messages: cheaper energy supports household cash flow, while weak commodity demand can reflect slower industrial activity. The following month's volumes would have to choose between them.

United States

Some categories still grew

US retail sales were flat, but some categories still grew

Payrolls increased by 64,000 in November. Health care and construction added jobs, while federal employment fell; unemployment rose to 4.6%. October retail and food-service sales were virtually unchanged from September and 3.5% above a year earlier. The strongest year-on-year categories included non-store retail at 9.0% and food services at 4.1%.

This mix matters because retail data cover goods and restaurants but not the full service economy. A household can postpone a vehicle or appliance while continuing to buy meals and online goods. Conversely, positive year-on-year values can coexist with a weakening monthly trajectory. I therefore read the report as a loss of momentum with remaining pockets of demand, not as evidence that consumption had already fallen across the board.

The Federal Reserve cut its target range to 3.5–3.75% on 10 December. The first beneficiaries would be borrowers using rates linked closely to short-term benchmarks. The households most sensitive to auto and mortgage approval still faced bank standards and collateral constraints documented in November. [Federal Reserve, 10 December]

My view

The delayed data gave a clearer view of the US, but they still did not show households cutting spending everywhere. I would worry more if restaurants and other discretionary services weakened together with goods, hours and payrolls. To call a recovery, I wanted to see monthly sales rise again rather than rely on year-on-year growth from earlier spending.

United Kingdom

Retail fell again

Lower inflation had not yet led households to buy more

GDP fell 0.1% in October and 0.1% over the three months to October. Services declined 0.3% in the month, while production rose 1.1%. The direction had therefore reversed from September, when services supported activity and motor manufacturing caused much of the output fall. November CPI slowed to 3.2% and services inflation to 4.4%. [ONS GDP, 12 December] [ONS CPI, 17 December]

Retail volumes fell 0.1% in November after a 0.9% decline in October. Supermarket volumes fell for a fourth consecutive month, and Black Friday provided a smaller lift than usual. This gave a more concrete household signal than the inflation rate. Prices rising more slowly improve purchasing power only gradually; the volume data showed that households had not yet used that improvement to buy more items. [ONS retail sales, 19 December]

The Bank of England cut Bank Rate to 3.75% by 5–4, with four members preferring no change. Fixed-rate mortgage schedules and bank repricing mean the cash-flow benefit will be distributed over time. [Bank of England, 18 December]

My view

Two monthly falls in retail volumes, including food, were harder to dismiss than one weak GDP reading. Before calling a consumer recovery, I wanted to see supermarket and discretionary purchases rise again. Inflation could keep falling and make goods more affordable, but demand would not have improved unless households actually bought more.

China

Property funding weakened

Housing weakened in sales, construction and funding

November industrial value added grew 4.8% from a year earlier and high-technology manufacturing 8.4%. Retail sales rose only 1.3%, while online physical-goods sales increased 5.7% over the first eleven months. The housing data helped explain why production could remain strong while households were more cautious.

Real-estate investment fell 15.9% in January–November, sales by value fell 11.1%, new starts fell 20.5%, and mortgages received by developers fell 15.1%. These measures cover separate parts of the cash cycle. Sales provide deposits and confidence; mortgage proceeds fund developers; starts create orders for construction materials and labour. Their simultaneous decline made the housing drag broader than a price or investment statistic alone. [NBS real estate, 18 December]

My view

The property data formed a difficult cycle: fewer sales reduced developers' cash, less funding limited new projects, and fewer projects reduced orders for materials and labour. Fast growth in high-technology manufacturing could continue at the same time, but it would not solve the housing problem. I wanted to see sales and developer funding improve before treating stronger industrial output as a wider recovery.

Underfollowed indicator

Mortgage receipts show how weaker home sales reach developers

Property investment is reported after many financing decisions have already been made. Mortgage money received by developers arrives earlier. When it falls, even a developer with land and approved projects may lack the cash to begin construction or pay suppliers. The 15.1% decline, together with weaker sales and starts, showed that the housing problem was already affecting funding and real work rather than only confidence.
