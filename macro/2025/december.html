Monthly Macro Highlights

Hiring, retail activity and Chinese property investment all weakened as the Federal Reserve and Bank of England cut rates.

December 2025Reporting window: 25 November–19 December 2025By Jiefu Zhang

The month in one view

December provides the clearest demand warning so far. US hiring slowed and October retail sales were flat, UK output and retail volumes fell, and China’s property contraction deepened. Rate cuts reduce restraint, but they do not reverse those decisions immediately.

Global

Demand weakens as policy turns less restrictive

US unemployment rose to 4.6%, UK output fell for a second month, and Chinese retail growth slowed to 1.3%. [BLS, 16 December] [ONS, 12 December] [NBS retail sales]

The broader evidence points the same way. US October retail sales were virtually unchanged, UK November retail volumes slipped 0.1%, and Chinese real-estate investment fell 15.9% over the first eleven months. [US Census, 16 December] [ONS retail sales, 19 December] [NBS real estate, 16 December]

My view

I now put more weight on a demand slowdown than I did in November because spending and investment have weakened alongside employment and output. December’s rate cuts may help borrowers as loans reprice, but the immediate question is whether households and firms want to borrow. If orders and retail volumes keep falling after the cuts, policy will be responding to weakness rather than starting a recovery.

Policy and market implications

Rate cuts arrive before demand recovers

The Federal Reserve lowered its target range to 3.5–3.75% on 10 December. The Bank of England cut Bank Rate to 3.75% eight days later by 5–4; four members preferred no change. [Federal Reserve, 10 December] [Bank of England, 18 December]

The cuts landed beside weak spending data rather than a visible rebound. US October retail sales were flat and UK November volumes fell 0.1%. [US Census, 16 December] [ONS, 19 December] Policy rates affect new loans quickly but fixed-rate mortgages and company debt only when they reset, so the transmission will be staggered.

My view

I treat the cuts as a response to weaker demand, not evidence that recovery has begun. The useful test is the price and availability of new credit, followed by actual borrowing and spending. A lower policy rate without stronger loan demand would offer cash-flow relief to some borrowers but little immediate lift to revenue.

United States

Headline flat, mix firmer

Flat sales conceal stronger online and restaurant spending

November payrolls rose by 64,000 and unemployment reached 4.6%. Health care and construction added jobs, while federal employment fell. [BLS, 16 December]

October retail and food-service sales were virtually unchanged from September and 3.5% higher than a year earlier. Non-store retailers were up 9.0% year on year, while food services rose 4.1%. [US Census, 16 December]

My view

Aggregate sales stalled, but the category detail was not uniformly weak: online and restaurant spending remained well above a year earlier. Households appear to have delayed some goods purchases rather than cut every form of consumption. A later fall in services spending would be a broader warning than flat headline retail sales alone.

United Kingdom

Consumer volumes fall

Output and retail volumes both soften

GDP fell 0.1% in October and over the latest three months. Services declined 0.3% in the month, while production rose 1.1%. November CPI inflation eased to 3.2% and services inflation to 4.4%. [ONS GDP, 12 December] [ONS CPI, 17 December]

Retail volumes fell 0.1% in November after a 0.9% October decline. Supermarket volumes fell for a fourth consecutive month, and Black Friday provided a smaller lift than usual. [ONS retail sales, 19 December]

My view

Lower inflation helps real income, but households were not yet converting that relief into stronger retail volumes. The December rate cut will also reach mortgages and business loans gradually. I would look for supermarket and discretionary volumes to turn before calling a consumer recovery; another better inflation print on its own would not show that demand has improved.

China

Housing drag deepens

Retail and property lose pace together

November industrial value added grew 4.8% year on year and high-technology manufacturing 8.4%, while retail sales rose only 1.3%. Online physical-goods sales were 5.7% higher over the first eleven months. [NBS industrial production] [NBS retail sales]

Housing weakened further. Real-estate investment fell 15.9% in January–November, sales by value fell 11.1%, new starts fell 20.5%, and mortgages received by developers fell 15.1%. [NBS real estate, 16 December]

My view

The December issue is not simply another comparison between factories and shoppers. Retail growth slowed while property sales, construction starts and developer funding all deteriorated. That combination matters because weaker housing can reduce household wealth and developers’ purchases at the same time. I would need to see sales and funding stabilise before treating industrial growth as a broad recovery.

What to watch

Where December’s slowdown should appear next

Credit transmission

Policy rates have fallen, but new-loan pricing, approval volumes and refinancing spreads will show who actually receives relief.

US retail breadth

Online and restaurant sales remained positive from a year earlier. A turn across discretionary categories would show whether weaker hiring is reaching spending.

UK household volumes

Supermarket and Black Friday volumes were soft. A recovery needs real volumes to improve, not only lower inflation.

China developer funding

Sales, mortgages received by developers and new starts are now weakening together. Funding stabilisation would be the first practical sign that the housing drag is easing.
