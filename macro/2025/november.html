Monthly Macro Highlights

Data return,
clarity does not

The US shutdown has left holes in the economic picture, UK growth is fading, and China’s industrial resilience still sits beside cautious domestic demand.

November 2025Data through 24 November 2025By Jiefu Zhang

The month in one view

November is a month for smaller conclusions. Delayed US figures, a one-off hit to British manufacturing and an uneven Chinese recovery all make the headlines less dependable than usual.

Global

Uncertainty is part of the data

When releases are delayed or distorted, confidence in precise forecasts should fall. September US payrolls have finally arrived, UK production is carrying a large motor-industry disruption, and China’s industrial growth conceals a manufacturing PMI of 49.0. The sensible response is to focus on direction, composition and the indicators that agree with one another.

The common thread is narrowness rather than recession. Employment gains are concentrated, UK services are doing just enough, and Chinese equipment and technology producers are stronger than the broader factory survey. Markets may keep rewarding the small group of assets with visible growth, but that concentration creates vulnerability if earnings disappoint.

My view

I would avoid building a strong portfolio view around a single month. Slower growth is visible, but the evidence is not clean enough to call a broad downturn. My base case is a low-growth expansion with wider dispersion between companies. I would change that view if US unemployment rises alongside weaker claims and spending, or if China’s new orders remain below the no-change line despite policy support. Until then, missing information argues for a higher bar for conviction, not automatic pessimism.

Markets and policy

Central banks are edging closer to the growth problem

The Bank of England held Bank Rate at 4% in November, but the vote was 5–4 and four members preferred a 25-basis-point cut. The Bank judged that inflation persistence had become less threatening while the risk from weaker demand was more apparent. [Bank of England, 6 November]

That close vote is a useful market signal. Bond yields can fall on weak activity while equities remain supported by the prospect of easier policy, but the tension lasts only while earnings hold up.

United States

Incomplete picture

September finally arrives

September payrolls rose by 119,000 and unemployment was 4.4%. July and August gains were revised down by a combined 33,000; no October employment report will be published because household data were not collected during the shutdown. [BLS, 20 November]

My view

The labour market appears softer than the payroll headline suggests because earlier months were revised down and unemployment edged higher. Missing October data increase the value of claims, surveys and company commentary, but each covers only part of the labour market. I would want several of them to weaken together before treating the slowdown as a sharper break.

United Kingdom

Growth fades

A small expansion with a large industrial interruption

GDP grew 0.1% in the third quarter but fell 0.1% in September. Motor-vehicle manufacturing dropped 28.6% in the month and was the largest drag, while services grew 0.2%. [ONS, 13 November]

My view

The production hit exaggerates the weakness, but 0.1% quarterly growth is hardly comfortable. Services are preventing contraction rather than generating a convincing recovery. That leaves the economy vulnerable: a modest decline in consumer or business services could remove the main source of growth.

China

Industry resilient

Advanced manufacturing keeps carrying the load

Industrial production grew 4.9% year on year in October. Equipment manufacturing rose 8.0% and high-tech manufacturing 7.2%, while the manufacturing PMI was 49.0. [NBS, 14 November]

My view

The strength is real but concentrated. Fast-growing equipment and technology output support selected companies, but a PMI below 50 suggests that many manufacturers are not sharing the improvement. Subdued household confidence adds another reason not to conclude that the wider economy is entering a strong upswing.

What I am watching

Finding a cleaner signal

US alternatives

Claims, vacancies and surveys can fill part of the shutdown gap. I am looking for agreement across them because none has the breadth of payrolls.

UK services

If new business and consumer-facing volumes weaken too, the argument that September was mainly a motor-industry shock becomes less persuasive.

China orders

Strong production without matching orders risks inventory and price pressure. Orders will show whether advanced manufacturing has enough demand behind it.

Revisions

First estimates are unusually easy to overinterpret. Downward revisions across several indicators would turn an untidy slowdown into a clearer warning.
