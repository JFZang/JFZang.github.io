Monthly Macro Highlights

Delayed US payrolls filled only part of the data gap, UK firms cut investment, and Chinese export growth shifted away from the United States towards ASEAN.

November 2025Reporting window: 28 October–24 November 2025By Jiefu Zhang

The month in one view

November moves from October’s data-visibility problem to decisions already being made: US banks tightened business credit, UK companies cut investment, and Chinese exporters found growth in ASEAN as trade with the United States contracted.

Global

Capital commitments weaken before headline activity

Investment and credit supplied the more forward-looking signals this month. UK business investment fell 0.3% in the third quarter, Chinese real-estate investment fell 14.7% in the first ten months, and US banks tightened commercial and industrial loan standards for firms of all sizes. [ONS business investment, 13 November] [NBS real estate, 15 November] [Federal Reserve lending survey, 3 November]

These decisions affect future capacity and hiring rather than only current output. The UK investment fall extended beyond September’s motor-industry disruption, Chinese developers reduced building activity, and small US firms did not report the increase in loan demand seen among larger companies.

My view

October left the cycle obscured by missing data; November shows caution in commitments made before demand appears in GDP. The common feature is not falling output but less willingness or ability to fund the next project. That shifts my attention from delayed releases to business investment, project starts and small-firm borrowing.

Policy and market implications

A close UK vote and tighter US business credit

The Bank of England held Bank Rate at 4% by 5–4, with four members preferring a quarter-point cut. The vote shows disagreement about the balance of risks, not a promise of a December move. [Bank of England, 6 November]

US bank lending was not easing uniformly either. The Fed’s October survey reported tighter standards for commercial and industrial loans to firms of all sizes. Demand strengthened among large and mid-sized firms but was unchanged among small firms; banks were less willing to approve borrowers with high trade exposure. [Federal Reserve lending survey, 3 November]

My view

The policy vote and the lending survey point in different directions. The Bank was close to cutting, yet US credit access was becoming less equal, especially for small or trade-exposed firms. I would not treat a lower policy rate as broad relief unless loan availability and borrowing demand improve beyond large companies.

United States

Labour and credit split

Payrolls return, but the credit data are split

September payrolls rose by 119,000 and unemployment was 4.4%. July and August gains were revised down by a combined 33,000; no October employment report will be published because household data were not collected during the shutdown. [BLS, 20 November]

Banks reported stronger demand for business loans from large and mid-sized firms but unchanged demand from small firms. Auto-loan demand weakened, while mortgage demand strengthened. [Federal Reserve lending survey, 3 November]

My view

The payroll report says hiring slowed; the credit survey says the slowdown is not uniform. Larger firms were borrowing more, partly for inventory and investment, while small-business and auto-loan demand lacked the same momentum. That split is more useful than calling the whole economy strong or weak. I would look for agreement between small-business hiring, loan demand and retail spending before making a sharper call.

United Kingdom

Output and capex soften

Motor output falls and business investment slips

GDP grew 0.1% in the third quarter but fell 0.1% in September. Motor-vehicle manufacturing dropped 28.6% in the month and was the largest drag, while services grew 0.2%. [ONS, 13 November]

Business investment fell 0.3% from the previous quarter, although it was 0.7% higher than a year earlier. [ONS business investment, 13 November]

My view

The motor-industry fall explains much of September’s production decline, but it does not explain weaker business investment. That makes the quarter harder to dismiss as one disrupted sector. Services are preventing contraction; a recovery would require firms to resume investment rather than merely avoid cutting output elsewhere.

China

Trade routes shift

Export growth shifts from the United States towards ASEAN

In the first ten months of 2025, goods exports rose 5.3% from a year earlier while imports fell 0.9%. The aggregate concealed a large change in destination: exports to the United States fell 17.8%, while exports to ASEAN rose 14.3% and exports to Africa rose 26.1%. [China Customs, 8 November]

The regional shift kept total exports growing despite the fall in US-bound shipments. Imports did not share that growth, so the trade data also show that stronger access to alternative markets was not matched by an equivalent rise in demand for foreign goods at home.

My view

The October trade data show adaptation, but not without a change in exposure. Exporters found growth in ASEAN and Africa as US trade contracted, increasing the importance of demand and trade policy in those markets. The next question is whether ASEAN-bound shipments reflect final demand there or the rerouting of supply chains serving other destinations.

What to watch

Four checks on November’s new signals

US small-business credit

Loan demand did not strengthen among small firms. Hiring, credit-line inquiries and approval standards will show whether the gap with larger companies widens.

UK investment

A second quarterly decline in business investment would show that caution extends beyond the motor-industry interruption.

China trade routes

Exports shifted towards ASEAN and Africa as US-bound shipments fell. Destination data and import demand will show whether the new geography is durable.

Delayed-data revisions

The next US releases will cover different reference periods. Direction across revisions matters more than comparing one delayed headline with a timely one.
