Monthly Macro Highlights

October 2026 Macro Highlights

October follows the split between stronger global goods trade and weaker services trade through supplier substitution, US capital-goods imports, UK export destinations, China's two-way electronics trade and market pricing.

October 2026Reporting window: 26 September–23 October 2026By Jiefu Zhang

Central question

Why did global goods trade strengthen without a broader recovery in demand?

Two concentrated changes lifted goods trade. Alternative suppliers replaced much of the oil, gas and fertiliser lost from the Middle East, while AI-related equipment moved rapidly through cross-border production chains. Travel and transport services could not adjust as easily. Stronger goods trade therefore showed that supply networks had adapted; it did not show that households and firms everywhere were spending more.

What changed this month

The outlook improved for goods and weakened for services

Goods trade forecast: 3.9%

The WTO raised its 2026 volume-growth forecast from 1.9% in March.

Services trade forecast: 3.3%

The WTO lowered its forecast from 4.8% as transport and travel weakened.

AI-enabling goods: +67% y/y

These products supplied 47% of global merchandise-trade growth in the first half.

Container throughput: +3.9%

Global throughput continued to rise through July despite disrupted energy routes.

Evidence and mechanism

Goods buyers could change suppliers; many services were simply lost

The WTO raised its forecast for 2026 merchandise-trade volume growth from 1.9% to 3.9%. It made the opposite change to commercial services, cutting expected growth from 4.8% to 3.3%, while forecasting world GDP growth of 2.6%. The difference matters because a stronger trade forecast can come from more final demand, a change in suppliers or more cross-border stages of production. October's report pointed mainly to the latter two. [WTO, 8 October]

Middle Eastern crude-oil exports fell by about 24% in the first half and LNG exports by 47%. Other producers replaced much of the shortfall, limiting the decline in global exports to around 6% for crude and 1% for LNG. Nitrogenous-fertiliser imports were only 2.8% below recent averages, while phosphatic-fertiliser imports were 2.2% higher. Buyers were still using energy and fertiliser; more of it came from a different country or travelled along a different route.

This substitution preserved trade volumes without requiring a new source of demand. It could also increase the distance travelled and the cost of moving the same quantity. Global container throughput rose 3.9% in the year to July, which showed that the transport network continued to move goods, not that the conflict had become economically harmless.

AI-related trade added a different effect. Semiconductors, servers and electrical equipment pass through design, fabrication, packaging, assembly and installation. Components can cross borders several times before the final equipment is used. Trade in AI-enabling goods increased 67% from a year earlier and accounted for 47% of global merchandise-trade growth in the first half. One investment project could therefore add to trade at several production stages without producing an equally large increase in final expenditure.

Transport and travel services had less room to adjust. A cancelled flight, hotel stay or shipment service could not simply be bought from another producer and stored for later. International tourist arrivals fell 0.8% from a year earlier in the second quarter, while travellers' expenditure growth slowed from 15% in the first quarter to 5%. Computer and financial services continued to grow, but did not fully offset the weaker location-dependent services.

The measures also require care. The WTO forecast refers to trade volumes. The national releases below mostly report current-price values, which also change with energy prices, component prices and exchange rates. They show where trade was concentrated, but cannot be treated as equivalent measures of physical quantity.

Answer

Goods trade strengthened because supply chains found replacement suppliers and AI equipment generated cross-border transactions at several stages of production. Services tied to transport, travel and location could not adjust in the same way. The change showed that the trading system had kept goods moving, but the concentration in AI equipment and the weakness in services argued against reading it as a broad recovery in final demand.

Market dashboard

Markets did not treat the trade upgrade as a broad demand boom

US 10-year yield: −24bp

The yield fell from 5.24% on 28 September to 5.00% on 23 October.

Nominal–TIPS gap: unchanged

The difference between the two ten-year yields remained about 2.34%.

High-yield effective yield: +11bp

A wider credit spread left the index yield higher despite lower Treasury yields.

S&P 500: −0.8%

The index fell from 7,683.69 to 7,623.68 over the reporting window.

The nominal ten-year yield and the ten-year TIPS yield each fell by 24 basis points, leaving their difference unchanged. The move did not resemble a large reduction in long-term inflation compensation. At the same time, the high-yield spread rose from 3.02% to 3.38%, and the effective yield increased from 8.03% to 8.14%. These prices did not prove why yields moved, but they placed a useful limit on the trade data: investors were not simultaneously pricing stronger trade, higher long-run inflation and lower corporate risk. United States

Demand centre and importer

Record capital-goods imports distributed part of US equipment demand abroad

US imports increased USD 17.2 billion in August, while exports rose USD 4.5 billion. The trade deficit widened by USD 12.7 billion to USD 105.6 billion, and capital-goods imports reached a record USD 146.4 billion. The services surplus was almost unchanged at USD 31.0 billion. [US Census and BEA, 6 October]

The record did not mean that every imported capital good was related to AI. It did show how strong equipment demand could lift global trade before appearing in US output. A data centre or factory can combine imported components with domestic construction, software and engineering. The imported equipment enters trade immediately, while the domestic benefits depend on how it is installed and used later.

US production showed the same concentration from the other side. Durable manufacturing output increased at a 3.8% annual rate in the third quarter, partly because semiconductor and related-component output rose 28.4%. Yet the broader business-equipment index was unchanged in September. Domestic semiconductor production was strong, but it was not evidence that all categories of business equipment had accelerated. [Federal Reserve, 16 October]

My view

The next step is to compare imported capital goods with domestic equipment production, rather than treating either as a complete measure of US investment. If both broaden, the current trade cycle will be adding to US productive capacity as well as overseas orders. If imports stay high while domestic output remains concentrated, the effect on global trade will be clearer than the effect on the wider US manufacturing base.

United Kingdom

EU exports remained weak

The global goods improvement did not reach the UK evenly

In the three months to August, UK goods exports fell 0.5% while goods imports rose 1.2%. The goods deficit widened by GBP 2.5 billion to GBP 59.1 billion. An estimated GBP 1.1 billion increase in the services surplus offset part of that change, leaving the total trade deficit at GBP 8.0 billion. [ONS, 15 October]

The geographic split was more informative than the total. Goods exports to the EU fell 3.5%, while exports to non-EU countries rose 2.3%. Non-EU imports increased 2.9%, while EU imports declined 0.4%. The UK was selling less into its largest nearby market at the same time as it bought more from faster-growing trade routes outside the EU.

This was consistent with the WTO's regional forecast: Asian merchandise exports were expected to rise 9.9% in 2026, while European exports were expected to fall 0.1%. The stronger global figure therefore did not imply the same export improvement for the UK. Product mix may also have mattered, but the monthly release did not provide enough evidence to assign the change to AI equipment or any single category.

The services estimate should also be treated cautiously. Monthly services data were partly projected from second-quarter information and could be revised. The widening surplus provided a buffer, but it did not yet show which UK services had benefited from the continued strength in digital and financial trade.

My view

The main UK test is whether exports to the EU recover. A continued rise in non-EU trade would not fully compensate if demand in the closer European market remained weak, especially when non-EU imports were also rising. This makes the destination of trade more useful than the headline export total for judging whether the global improvement is reaching UK producers.

China

Both supplier and buyer

Fast imports showed that the electronics chain ran in both directions

China's goods trade increased 18.8% from a year earlier in the first nine months of 2026. Exports rose 16.4% and imports 22.3%. In September alone, exports increased 29.6% and imports 24.6%. These were current-price renminbi values, so they included changes in prices and product mix as well as quantities. [China customs data, 14 October]

Trade in industrial robots, electronic components, computer parts and electrical equipment exceeded RMB 10 trillion in the first three quarters. It rose 58.9% and contributed 11.3 percentage points to total trade growth. This gave the global AI effect a direct Chinese counterpart, but the import growth changed the interpretation. China was buying components and equipment as well as exporting finished and intermediate goods.

A component can be imported for processing and then included in an exported product. Both transactions enter gross trade, even though they support the same final item. This does not make the growth artificial, but it explains why a cross-border production chain can lift trade much faster than final demand. It also means that exports alone give an incomplete picture of China's role in the cycle.

My view

I would watch whether electronics imports continue to grow with exports. Continued growth on both sides would suggest that China remained integrated into an expanding production chain. Strong exports alongside weaker related imports would point more towards greater use of existing capacity or domestic substitution. That distinction is more useful than treating every increase in the trade surplus as the same economic development.

Since last month

The energy shock changed trade routes more than global goods volumes

What continued

Transport and travel services remained more exposed to the Middle East disruption than digital services.

What changed

What the new data added

What would change my view
