Monthly Macro Highlights

Several indicators improved after December's weak readings, but the longer comparisons were still soft. The question was whether this was the start of a recovery or simply one better month.

January 2026Reporting window: 20 December 2025–23 January 2026By Jiefu Zhang

Central question

Did December's weakness mark a slide into contraction, or a temporary pause?

January's releases were better than December's. US retail sales rose, UK monthly GDP and retail volumes recovered, and China reached 5% annual growth. But one stronger month was not enough to show a new upturn: UK retail volumes still fell over the quarter, Chinese growth slowed in the fourth quarter, and household spending there rose less than income.

What changed this month

Monthly data improved before the longer trend did

US nominal retail sales: +0.6% m/m

November receipts recovered from October's flat reading; non-store and restaurant sales remained stronger than the aggregate over the year.

UK monthly GDP: +0.3% m/m

November output rebounded, but growth over the latest three months was only 0.1%.

UK Q4 retail volumes: −0.3% q/q

A 0.4% December rise did not prevent volumes from falling across the quarter.

China 2025 real income: +5.0%

Household consumption expenditure rose 4.4% in real terms, leaving income growth ahead of spending.

Evidence and mechanism

One better month looked different from a full quarter

A monthly series is useful for detecting a turn, but it is also more exposed to timing, weather and individual industries. The UK made the problem visible. GDP rose 0.3% in November after two weak months, yet output increased only 0.1% over the three-month period. Retail volumes rose 0.4% in December but fell 0.3% over the fourth quarter. The positive observations were real; they were not large enough to repair the preceding losses. [ONS GDP, 15 January] [ONS retail sales, 23 January]

US retail sales gave clearer evidence of an improvement. November sales rose 0.6% from October and 3.3% from a year earlier, with online retail up 7.2% and food services up 4.9% over the year. December payrolls rose only 50,000, however. Spending can continue for a while when hiring slows because existing workers still receive wages, households use savings or purchases are brought forward. Hours worked and income still needed to improve if the retail gain was to last. [US Census, 14 January] [BLS, 9 January]

China's annual data raised a different question. GDP increased 5.0% in 2025, but fourth-quarter growth slowed to 4.5%. Real household disposable income rose 5.0%, while real consumption expenditure increased 4.4%. Households therefore saved part of the income gain, and property income rose only 1.6%. Housing and wealth concerns may explain some of that caution, even though spending on education, culture and recreation grew faster.

Answer

December was not the start of an uninterrupted decline. US retail sales and UK output both improved, and China still recorded positive annual growth. But the stronger readings had not yet spread: the UK's quarterly figures remained weak, US hiring added little momentum, and Chinese household spending grew more slowly than income. January reduced the immediate concern without showing a clear recovery.

Market dashboard

Credit markets calmed down while oil prices rose

S&P 500: +0.5%

The index moved from 6,878.49 on 22 December to 6,915.61 on 23 January, broadly matching the modest economic improvement.

High-yield spread: −20bp

The spread narrowed from 2.88% to 2.68%, signalling easier market finance despite weak hiring.

VIX: 16.09

Volatility rose from 14.08 and briefly reached 20.09 on 20 January before receding.

Brent: +9.5%

Spot Brent rose from USD 62.22 to USD 68.16, reversing December's energy relief.

[FRED, S&P 500] [FRED, high-yield spread] [FRED, VIX] [FRED, Brent]

The market data were mixed. Credit spreads narrowed and equities ended slightly higher, but volatility remained above its starting point and oil rose sharply. Lower spreads helped companies able to issue bonds, but said little about bank loans for small firms. If the oil increase continued, it could also remove part of the household benefit from slower goods inflation.

United States

Retail sales improved

Retail sales improved even though hiring remained weak

December payrolls rose 50,000 and unemployment fell to 4.4%. Job creation remained concentrated in health care and social assistance, while professional and business services and federal employment declined. December CPI was 2.7% from a year earlier. The combination of modest hiring and lower inflation could preserve real income, but it did not show that labour demand had accelerated. [BLS CPI, 13 January]

November retail sales rose 0.6%, with online and restaurant spending still leading annual growth. This improvement mattered after October's flat result. I wanted to see whether it continued together with hours worked and wage income. If spending rose while hours fell, households might be relying on savings or debt rather than earning more.

My view

January's data did not show a broad fall in US household spending. The retail improvement gave the labour market more time to stabilise, but it could not replace wage income for long. I would feel more confident if hours worked and hiring improved without another rise in inflation. Downward sales revisions or faster consumer borrowing without stronger income would make me more cautious.

United Kingdom

Monthly rise, weak quarter

The monthly rebound did not repair the weak quarter

November GDP grew 0.3%, supported by a 0.3% rise in services, while construction fell 1.3%. Output increased only 0.1% over the latest three months. December retail volumes rose 0.4% but remained down 0.3% over the quarter; annual volumes were 1.3% above 2024 but still below 2019. These comparisons answer different questions. The month captured a rebound; the quarter and long-run level showed how little ground had been recovered.

December CPI was 3.4% and services inflation 4.5%. Higher services prices alongside weak quarterly volumes did not imply strong demand by itself. Wage contracts, rents and regulated or administered charges can keep prices rising after customers reduce purchases. The rate cut made in December would also reach fixed-rate borrowers at different refinancing dates.

My view

The November GDP and December retail gains were real improvements, so I would not dismiss them. They were still too small to offset weak quarterly retail, construction and the longer comparison with 2019. Another month of broader growth in services and goods, followed by stronger business investment, would make the rebound more convincing.

China

Income grew faster than spending

Household income grew faster than spending

GDP grew 5.0% in 2025; fourth-quarter growth slowed to 4.5%. Manufacturing expanded 6.1% across the year, while construction contracted 1.1%. The national total therefore met the stated growth target through different contributions, and the late-year pace was weaker than the annual average. [NBS GDP, 21 January]

The household accounts made the behaviour more concrete. Real disposable income increased 5.0%, while real consumption expenditure rose 4.4%. Property income grew only 1.6%. Housing expenditure rose 2.1%, compared with 9.4% for education, culture and recreation. Households were not uniformly cutting spending: they increased selected services rapidly but did not spend their full real-income gain. [NBS household survey, 20 January]

My view

The interesting part of the Chinese data was the gap between income and spending. Weak property income and falling housing activity help explain why households kept more of what they earned. I would see a clearer change if the share of income being spent rose together with property transactions. Reaching the same GDP target again would tell us less about household confidence.

Underfollowed indicator

A three-month comparison helps check a strong monthly number

Monthly data detect change quickly, but a single rebound can be generated by a postponed purchase, a factory reopening or normal volatility. Comparing the month with the latest three months asks whether the new direction is large enough to alter the accumulated result. January's UK data offered a textbook example: GDP rose 0.3% in November but only 0.1% over three months; retail volumes rose 0.4% in December but fell 0.3% in the quarter. A durable turn should improve both frequencies in sequence.

Since last month

January improved on December, but did not settle the question

What improved

US retail sales and UK monthly output rose after December's weaker readings.

What remained weak

UK quarterly retail volumes and China's household spending relative to income had not improved.

What changed

What would change my view

Several months of gains that also lifted quarterly volumes, investment and income would show a wider recovery.
