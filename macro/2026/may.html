Monthly Macro Highlights

US inflation rose as banks reported weaker consumer credit demand, UK services and investment expanded, and Chinese industrial profits improved mainly in technology and materials.

May 2026Reporting window: 25 April–22 May 2026By Jiefu Zhang

The month in one view

May reveals different kinds of strength. US nominal spending grew but credit demand weakened, UK services and business investment improved together, and Chinese profit growth was concentrated in electronics and materials rather than spread across manufacturing.

Global

Credit, investment and profits point in different directions

US banks reported weaker demand for consumer loans, UK business investment rose 0.7% from the previous quarter, and Chinese electronics profits rose rapidly while auto and building-material profits fell. [Federal Reserve lending survey, 4 May] [ONS business investment, 14 May] [NBS industrial profits, 28 April]

The headline data supplied context rather than a common direction. US inflation rose as payroll growth slowed, UK services led a 0.6% quarterly GDP gain, and Chinese industrial output grew 4.1%. [BLS employment, 8 May] [BLS CPI, 12 May] [ONS GDP, 14 May]

My view

The balance-sheet data matter more this month than a single output comparison. US consumer borrowing softened, UK firms added capital spending, and Chinese profits moved towards electronics and materials. The next round of activity will therefore be constrained in different places: US financed purchases and weaker Chinese industries, rather than every firm or household at once.

Policy and market implications

Bank standards tighten without a policy-rate move

The Federal Reserve and Bank of England both kept policy rates unchanged in late April. Their later inflation data pointed in different directions: US inflation rose, while UK inflation fell with household energy prices. [Federal Reserve, 29 April] [Bank of England, 30 April]

US banks nevertheless tightened standards for commercial and industrial loans and reported weaker demand for credit cards, auto loans and other consumer loans. Financing conditions therefore changed for some borrowers even though the policy rate did not. [Federal Reserve lending survey, 4 May]

My view

An unchanged policy rate did not mean unchanged credit conditions. Tighter business standards and weaker demand for financed household purchases can slow autos, equipment and other rate-sensitive spending before the central bank moves. Loan approvals and balances will show that transmission more directly than the next policy vote.

United States

Spending up, credit softer

Inflation rises while consumer credit demand weakens

April payrolls rose by 115,000 and unemployment stayed at 4.3%. CPI rose 0.6% in the month and 3.8% over the year; shelter rose 0.6%, and core CPI increased 0.4%. [BLS employment, 8 May] [BLS CPI, 12 May]

Retail and food-service sales rose 0.5% in April and 4.9% from a year earlier. Non-store sales were up 11.1% year on year, but banks reported weaker demand for credit cards and auto loans. [US Census, 14 May] [Federal Reserve lending survey, 4 May]

My view

Nominal spending is still growing, but higher shelter and energy prices make the real volume harder to read. Weaker consumer-credit demand adds a second caution: households may be spending from income while avoiding larger financed purchases. That is slower rotation, not yet contraction, but it makes autos and other credit-sensitive categories more vulnerable.

United Kingdom

Services and capex improve

Services and business investment grow together

GDP grew 0.6% in the first quarter, led by a 0.8% rise in services across most subsectors. April CPI inflation fell to 2.8% as household energy prices declined. [ONS GDP, 14 May] [ONS CPI, 20 May]

Business investment increased 0.7% from the previous quarter, although it was 1.8% below a year earlier. [ONS business investment, 14 May]

My view

The capital-spending rise matters because it accompanied growth across most service industries, not because inflation fell. Investment was still below a year earlier, so firms may have resumed delayed projects rather than expanded capacity. New orders and another quarter of capital spending will separate those explanations.

China

Profit gains concentrate

Industrial profits rise, but the gains are concentrated

April industrial value added grew 4.1% year on year, including 12.8% in high-technology manufacturing. Retail sales rose 0.2%, or 1.8% excluding autos; autos, appliances, furniture and building materials all declined. [NBS industrial production] [NBS retail sales]

First-quarter industrial profits rose 15.5%, but the sector spread was wide. Electronics and non-ferrous-metal profits each rose 120%, while auto profits fell 17.7% and non-metallic-mineral profits 42.6%. [NBS industrial profits, 28 April]

My view

Profit data answer a different question from output: some of the industrial growth is reaching company earnings. It is not broad. Technology and materials captured most of the increase, while autos and construction-linked products weakened. I would treat the profit rebound as real but concentrated, not as evidence that manufacturing or domestic demand has improved across the board.

What to watch

Where May’s divergence should show up

US financed purchases

Auto and consumer-loan demand weakened while nominal retail sales rose. Vehicle, furniture and other credit-sensitive categories will show which signal leads.

UK capital spending

Business investment rose for the quarter but remained lower than a year earlier. Follow-through would show that firms are moving beyond delayed projects.

China profit breadth

Technology and materials drove the profit gain. Inventories, receivables and profits in autos and construction-linked industries will show whether it spreads.

Bank-dependent demand

Banks tightened business standards and reported weaker consumer-loan demand. Approvals and outstanding balances will show whether caution becomes an actual fall in credit.
