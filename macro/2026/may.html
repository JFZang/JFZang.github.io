Monthly Macro Highlights

Credit had not dried up across the economy. The difficulty was concentrated among smaller firms, property developers, non-bank lenders and households trying to finance large purchases.

May 2026Reporting window: 25 April–22 May 2026By Jiefu Zhang

Central question

Who was finding it harder to borrow?

The pressure was concentrated rather than economy-wide. US banks charged more or demanded stricter collateral from riskier businesses and non-bank lenders. Small firms borrowed less from large banks, and households showed less demand for financed purchases. Chinese property developers lost loan and mortgage-related funding much faster than total investment fell. Total debt still looked stable, so these differences were easy to miss.

What changed this month

Loan terms showed where borrowing was becoming harder

Riskier business terms tightened

US banks reported higher risk premiums, tighter covenants and stricter collateral requirements even as some large-firm loan spreads eased.

Consumer demand weakened

Demand fell for credit cards, autos and other consumer loans; standards tightened for the residual consumer-loan category.

Annualised mortgage transition: 1.48%

The share of balances newly entering serious delinquency rose from 1.22% a year earlier; originations held at USD 530 billion.

China Jan–Apr developer funding: −18.4%

Domestic loans, advance receipts and individual mortgage proceeds all contracted faster than overall fixed investment.

Evidence and mechanism

Banks were becoming more selective about whom they would lend to

The Federal Reserve's April lending survey showed a modest tightening in commercial and industrial standards for firms of all sizes. Banks charged riskier borrowers more, tightened covenants and collateral requirements, and used interest-rate floors more often for some loans. Large banks also reported weaker demand from small firms, while demand from large companies was broadly unchanged. [Federal Reserve, 4 May]

Construction and non-bank borrowers faced some of the clearest restrictions. Demand for construction and land-development loans weakened, particularly outside the largest banks. Banks also tightened every category of lending to non-depository financial institutions, using shorter maturities, smaller credit lines and stricter collateral. These terms can cause the riskiest new projects to be delayed before total bank lending begins to fall. [Federal Reserve survey detail]

Household debt did not show a general freeze. Total debt rose 0.1% in Q1 to USD 18.79 trillion, mortgage originations were USD 530 billion and total delinquency changed little. However, the annualised flow into serious delinquency rose from 1.22% to 1.48% for mortgages and from 0.88% to 1.15% for home-equity lines. Repayment problems were increasing among some borrowers even though the total stock of debt remained stable. [New York Fed, 12 May]

The 1.48% figure has a narrow meaning. It is the annualised share of mortgage balances that newly moved into 90-plus-day delinquency during the quarter, not the share of all mortgages already seriously delinquent. The flow could therefore worsen while the aggregate delinquency stock changed little.

Answer

Credit was first becoming harder for borrowers that needed more collateral, depended on property cash flows or were already viewed as risky. This affected small firms, developers, non-bank lenders and some households before it reduced total lending. Aggregate debt was still too stable to call a credit crunch, but the detailed loan terms showed where activity could weaken next.

United States

Riskier borrowers faced tougher terms

Banks tightened some terms while borrowers also pulled back

Banks tightened business standards, but they did not report a uniform withdrawal. Some loan spreads for large companies eased, while risk premiums, covenants and collateral requirements became less favourable. Consumer credit demand weakened across cards, autos and other loans, and mortgage demand was softest in lower-quality categories. [Federal Reserve, 4 May]

The New York Fed's household panel showed a similar split. Card balances fell seasonally by USD 25 billion, while auto balances rose USD 18 billion and mortgage balances rose USD 21 billion. Credit limits and new loans were still available, but the flow into serious delinquency had increased over the year for mortgages, HELOCs, student loans, autos and cards. [New York Fed, 12 May]

My view

US credit was becoming more selective rather than shutting down. Large listed companies could still borrow in the market, while banks demanded more from riskier firms and households showed less interest in financed purchases. If this continued, I expected it to appear first in approvals, new projects and repayment problems rather than in a sudden fall in total debt.

United Kingdom

More credit, still expensive

UK lenders offered more credit, but borrowing was still expensive

The Bank of England's April report said lenders had increased the availability of secured household and corporate credit in the first quarter. The provisional estimate of UK business investment also rose 0.7% from the previous quarter, although it remained 1.8% below a year earlier. [Bank of England, 30 April] [ONS, 14 May]

The same report warned that higher mortgage rates were beginning to weigh on housing activity and that the full effect would arrive with a lag. More credit being offered is not the same as more credit being affordable or demanded.

My view

The UK showed why I did not want to describe this as the same credit squeeze everywhere. Lenders said more credit was available and business investment rose slightly, but higher mortgage rates still reduced demand. The problem was not only whether banks could lend; it was whether households and firms could afford and justify the loan.

China

Developer funding fell

Developer funding fell faster than property investment

From January to April, funds available to real-estate developers fell 18.4% from a year earlier. Domestic loans dropped 25.9%, deposits and advance receipts 17.6%, and individual mortgage proceeds 31.7%. Real-estate investment fell 13.7% and new starts 22.0%. [NBS real estate, 19 May]

Across the wider economy, fixed investment fell 1.6%, but non-governmental investment fell 5.2% while state-holding investment rose 2.5%. Equipment purchases increased 11.5%, showing that credit-sensitive construction and private projects were weaker than every form of capital spending. [NBS fixed investment, 19 May]

My view

China showed most clearly where limited funding was already affecting real projects. Developer cash and private investment fell, while equipment purchases in selected areas still rose. Credit was therefore restricting property and private projects rather than stopping every type of investment.

Underfollowed indicator

Loan terms can weaken before total lending falls

Total outstanding credit changes slowly because it includes loans agreed in earlier periods. Covenants, collateral requirements, interest-rate floors and maximum credit-line sizes describe what a new borrower faces now. A firm can delay a project after receiving worse terms even while total lending continues to grow. This is why the details of the loan contract mattered more than the headline balance in May.

Since last month

April's market rebound did not make borrowing easier for everyone

What continued

Equities and traded high-yield debt remained firm after April's recovery.

What changed

Bank surveys and developer funding showed that large borrowers had easier access than smaller or riskier ones.

Still unclear

Stable total debt did not show whether weaker approvals would later reduce spending and investment.

What would change my view

Stronger loan growth, easier terms and more private project starts would make the restrictions look temporary.
