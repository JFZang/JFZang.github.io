Monthly Macro Highlights

US payrolls recovered and core inflation eased, while weaker household loan demand and a drop in UK business investment kept the improvement out of credit-sensitive activity.

February 2026Reporting window: 24 January–20 February 2026By Jiefu Zhang

The month in one view

February improves the near-term US inflation and jobs trade-off, while adding two cautions outside the usual headlines: US banks report weaker household credit demand, and UK business investment fell sharply in the fourth quarter.

Global

Better US data do not reach credit-sensitive demand

US payroll growth recovered and core inflation eased to 2.5%. The UK avoided contraction by 0.1% in the fourth quarter, while China’s manufacturing and composite PMIs fell below 50. [BLS employment, 11 February] [BLS CPI, 13 February] [ONS, 12 February] [NBS PMI, 2 February]

Credit and investment were less encouraging. US banks reported weaker demand for most mortgages, auto loans and other consumer loans in the fourth quarter. UK business investment fell 2.7% from the previous quarter, although it remained 2.0% higher than a year earlier. [Federal Reserve lending survey, 2 February] [ONS business investment, 12 February]

My view

February separates current income from willingness to finance large purchases. US hiring improved and inflation eased, but mortgage and auto-loan demand fell while UK firms cut capital spending. The immediate data improved without evidence that households or firms had become readier to borrow; financing decisions, not another inflation print, are the more useful test.

Policy and market implications

Bank lending looks weaker than the policy vote

The Bank of England held Bank Rate at 3.75% by 5–4, with four members preferring a cut. It expected inflation to move towards 2% from April but remained concerned about wage and services persistence. [Bank of England, 5 February]

The Fed’s lending survey shows why the policy rate is only part of the transmission. Banks tightened business-loan standards, while mortgage, auto and other consumer-loan demand weakened. They also reported being more likely to approve loans to firms benefiting from AI and less likely to approve firms adversely exposed to it. [Federal Reserve lending survey, 2 February]

My view

The lending survey’s AI result is about credit allocation, not another growth forecast. Banks were already incorporating that exposure into approval decisions: firms judged to benefit received easier access than firms judged vulnerable to it. Financing gaps can therefore widen before aggregate investment changes, through lender risk assessment rather than capital spending alone.

United States

Jobs improve, credit softens

Jobs recover, but household credit demand weakens

January payrolls rose by 130,000 and unemployment was 4.3%. January CPI rose 0.2% on the month and 2.4% over the year; core inflation was 2.5%. [BLS, 11 February] [BLS, 13 February]

Banks nevertheless reported weaker fourth-quarter demand for most residential mortgages, auto loans and other consumer loans. They expected credit quality to deteriorate in residential real estate and most consumer categories during 2026. [Federal Reserve lending survey, 2 February]

My view

The jobs and CPI combination reduces the immediate conflict between the Fed’s objectives. The lending survey stops me from calling it a broad household acceleration: people were less willing to borrow for homes and cars, and banks expected consumer credit quality to worsen. Employment income can sustain spending, but credit-sensitive demand remains exposed.

United Kingdom

Investment contracts

A 0.1% expansion sits beside falling business investment

GDP grew 0.1% in the fourth quarter and 1.3% across 2025. December output rose 0.1%, with services growth offset by falls in production and construction. January CPI inflation fell to 3.0%. [ONS GDP, 12 February] [ONS CPI, 18 February]

Business investment fell 2.7% in the fourth quarter. It was still 2.0% above a year earlier, so the quarterly fall is a warning rather than proof of a lasting retreat. [ONS business investment, 12 February]

My view

The economy expanded 0.1%, but firms cut investment over the quarter. Lower inflation and December’s rate cut may improve cash flow, but neither forces companies to expand capacity when demand is uncertain. I would look for the investment decline to reverse before reading the GDP gain as the start of stronger momentum.

China

Prices turn before orders

Factory prices improve before orders do

January’s manufacturing PMI fell to 49.3 and the composite PMI to 49.8. New orders and employment were both below 50. Consumer inflation was 0.2% year on year, while producer prices fell 1.4% from a year earlier but rose 0.4% on the month. [NBS PMI] [NBS CPI] [NBS PPI]

Full-year industrial profits rose only 0.6% in 2025. Manufacturing profits increased 5.0%, but private-enterprise profits were flat and the average collection period for receivables lengthened by 3.6 days. [NBS industrial profits, 28 January]

My view

The monthly rise in producer prices is helpful, but it arrived before a clear recovery in orders or cash conversion. Flat private-sector profits and slower receivables collection show why a price turn should not be confused with a broad business recovery. I would wait for combined January–February orders, selling prices and receivables before calling an inflection.

What to watch

The next evidence should come from balance sheets

US consumer credit

Banks expect weaker credit quality in several household categories. Delinquencies and financed purchases will test whether that concern reaches actual spending.

UK business investment

The quarterly fall was large despite a positive annual comparison. The next estimate will show whether firms delayed projects or changed plans.

China cash conversion

Receivables are taking longer to collect. New orders, selling prices and collection days together will show whether better producer prices improve cash flow.

AI credit allocation

Banks favour firms that benefit from AI. Loan growth and capital spending outside that group will show whether investment is broadening or concentrating.
