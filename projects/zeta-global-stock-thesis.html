Strong demand is visible. The question is whether media-heavy growth can become per-share cash value.

Zeta's demand case rests on two disclosed indicators: management attributed 63.9% of FY2025 incremental revenue to existing customers, and Zeta reported that its super-scaled cohort—responsible for 87% of FY2025 revenue—continued to expand in both customer count and ARPU through Q2 FY2026. Its earnings profile is much less mature. Zeta only crossed annual GAAP operating break-even in FY2025, recording $5.4m of operating income on $1.305bn of revenue; after a Q1 FY2026 net loss, Q2 returned to $16.9m of operating income and $8.2m of net income. Strong demand and emerging GAAP profitability are the combination that often drives a growth-stock rerating: investors are willing to pay for revenue growth when it begins to convert into operating leverage. Zeta is not a clean software version of that setup, however, because media-heavy revenue, stock-based compensation and working-capital requirements can weaken the conversion from growth into per-share cash value.

I rate Zeta Neutral / Hold, with a $31 twelve-month target against a $29.07 reference price, implying approximately 6.2% upside. The target is probability-weighted; the standalone Base case is worth $33.98. That Base is not conservative: it forecasts consolidated revenue rising from $1.818bn in FY2026 to $3.768bn in FY2030 and forecast GAAP EBIT margin increasing from 3.0% to 14.4%. Achieving it requires approximately 20% revenue CAGR and S&M and G&A growing materially more slowly than revenue, even as gross margin edges down and stock-based compensation remains a real shareholder cost. Expense ratios have normalised materially from the FY2021–FY2022 post-IPO spike, but S&M remains above its FY2020 level and the Base requires G&A to move well below its pre-IPO ratio. The analyst scenario weighting therefore assigns 30% to Bear, pulling the probability-weighted value down to $30.87, rounded to $31. The Hold rating reflects execution-weighted valuation risk—not a pessimistic demand forecast.

What Zeta does—and why its revenue is not pure software

Zeta helps large enterprises decide which consumers to reach, what message or offer to deliver, where to deliver it, and how to measure the result. A customer can bring first-party data into the Zeta Marketing Platform, combine it with Zeta's identity and behavioural data, build audiences, automate decisions, send communications through owned channels such as email, or activate paid media across third-party inventory. Zeta therefore sits across several layers that are often supplied separately: customer data and identity, decisioning, journey orchestration, messaging, media activation and measurement. Zeta FY2025 Form 10-K.

That breadth makes the revenue model economically mixed. Zeta earns subscription fees, volume-based utilisation revenue and professional-services fees. Some activity is delivered directly through Zeta's own platform; other activity requires third-party publishers, media inventory, data or channel partners. The first category can resemble software, while paid-media activation may include substantial third-party delivery cost. Consequently, a dollar of additional Zeta revenue does not always carry the same gross margin, and reported revenue can grow faster than gross profit when customer spending shifts toward media.

Two M&A transactions materially widened this model. Zeta purchased LiveIntent in October 2024, adding people-based identity, audience-building, media-activation and publisher-monetisation capabilities. It purchased the Marigold Enterprise Business in November 2025, adding email, loyalty and customer-engagement products together with a large international installed base. These transactions increased Zeta's exposure to both media activation and more subscription-oriented messaging. Athena is intended to strengthen the intelligence and decisioning layer and could provide a more software-oriented counterweight. Zeta FY2025 Form 10-K; Zeta Q2 FY2026 earnings supplemental. Zeta is therefore neither a pure SaaS vendor nor a conventional advertising intermediary.

For the industry economics behind software, identity, messaging and media activation, see the companion Enterprise Marketing Data and Activation industry research.

Investment conclusion

The central investment question can be stated more precisely than “growth versus margin”:

Is Zeta increasing customer wallet share through media and activation, and can the resulting lower-margin revenue still create enough after-S&M contribution, cash conversion and per-share value?

This formulation matters because Zeta combines software, data, owned-channel messaging and paid-media activation. An existing customer can buy more from Zeta, lifting NRR and ARPU, while shifting the revenue mix toward services with higher third-party media cost. In that case, demand quality may improve while incremental gross margin falls. The direction of the economics cannot be inferred from either KPI alone.

The most useful disclosed approximation is therefore:

Revenue retained after delivery cost and incremental S&M = Incremental gross margin − incremental S&M cost ratio

This spread is an incremental commercial-contribution proxy: it tests whether each additional revenue dollar retains more or less value after delivery cost and incremental S&M.

Three conclusions follow.

  1. Demand among Zeta's large customers remains strong. Both the size of the super-scaled cohort and spending per customer continued to rise through Q2 FY2026.
  2. The quality of incremental revenue weakened in H1 FY2026. Delivery economics and incremental commercial burden both moved the wrong way; Marigold, seasonality and mix add noise, but do not make the signal irrelevant.
  3. The valuation depends on expenses growing more slowly than revenue, not on a higher gross margin. The Base keeps R&D near its current share of revenue and continues to fund equipment, data and software. Most of the profit improvement therefore comes from S&M and G&A becoming smaller percentages of revenue.

The history establishes demand, but not yet mature economics. Zeta's post-IPO organisation was built well ahead of revenue, and later acquisitions enlarged the cost base again. The Base asks that this investment finally convert: gross margin softens and R&D remains funded, so the improvement cannot come from a more favourable product mix or a thinner product roadmap. It must come mainly from serving a larger revenue base with proportionally less S&M and G&A. That direction has precedent; sustaining it across a business that now contains more media activity, more products and more international infrastructure is the real underwriting decision.

The useful signal is not simply that growth was high. Zeta is getting a large share of new revenue from customers it has already implemented, which makes continued wallet-share expansion more plausible than a forecast built entirely on new logos. It also means the mix deserves scrutiny: an installed customer can buy more media activation, lifting revenue and retention while contributing fewer gross-profit cents per dollar.

The bridge cannot cleanly produce an organic-growth series because political activity and incompletely disclosed M&A revenue remain mixed into some periods. The model therefore forecasts consolidated revenue and uses the customer bridge as a reasonableness check. The FY2025 Form 10-K and Q2 FY2026 earnings supplemental support the table.

The Zeta margin paradox

FY2025 NRR was 128%, versus 120% excluding LiveIntent. Both readings show that the starting customer cohort spent more, while the gap indicates that the media-heavy acquisition influenced the reported metric. Higher NRR can therefore coexist with lower incremental gross margin: customers may become more embedded by buying more activation and media, even though more of each revenue dollar passes to third parties.

The offset should appear in selling efficiency. Expanding an implemented account ought to require less incremental S&M than winning and onboarding a new one. The useful test is therefore the spread between incremental gross margin and the incremental S&M cost ratio. A lower incremental gross margin is economically acceptable if S&M falls by more. If both measures deteriorate, Zeta is adding lower-margin revenue without obtaining the expected cross-sell or scale benefit.

FY2021–FY2023 incremental ratios are poor operating benchmarks because large post-IPO cost resets dominate the calculation. The recent sequence is more useful: FY2025 combined unusually good media-cost conversion with low incremental S&M, but H1 FY2026 reversed both. That does not prove the installed-customer strategy is failing; it shows that revenue mix and selling efficiency have to be read together.

The Base assumes a partial recovery, not a return to the FY2025 peak. That is plausible if more spending comes from already implemented customers and the enlarged sales organisation begins to carry more revenue. It is not automatic: if media-heavy growth continues to need nearly the same commercial effort as a new deployment, the retained-revenue proxy will remain depressed even while NRR and headline revenue look healthy.

The acquisitions improve the credibility of high revenue growth because Zeta now has more products and a larger installed base. They do not automatically improve economics. LiveIntent appears to have performed strongly enough to raise the expected earn-out, but it also reinforces the lower-margin media mix. Marigold brings higher-gross-margin products, yet its pre-deal carve-out carried weak operating leverage and high R&D and D&A. The combined thesis is therefore cross-sell plus cost consolidation, not simply adding the two companies' revenue.

This distinction also explains the forecast. The Base includes the acquired revenue and gradual S&M and G&A absorption, but no further transaction. It preserves R&D because Marigold's products still require maintenance, migration and support. The key evidence is whether retention holds while duplicated sales coverage, infrastructure and corporate functions decline as a share of the enlarged revenue base.

The forecast uses one consolidated revenue line and assumes no further M&A. FY2026 is distorted upward by a full year of Marigold against only a short post-close contribution in FY2025; the later path is a set of explicit annual judgements rather than a mechanical organic-growth series. Political years receive a modest offset, but not enough to manufacture a precise cycle from incomplete disclosure.

The Base sits well above management's FY2028 minimum. The evidence supporting that gap is the combination of installed-customer expansion, a growing large-customer cohort and more products available for cross-sell—not a simple continuation of acquisition-affected growth. The harder assumption is duration. Even as the growth rate declines, the annual revenue dollars that Zeta must add keep rising. Without another acquisition or a measurable Athena contribution, the company has to keep deepening existing relationships while still adding new ones. That is plausible, but it is the part of the revenue case that deserves the highest burden of proof.

Athena is an upside option, not a Base-case plug

Athena could shift more of Zeta's value toward software-like decisioning and automation, but paid adoption, pricing and implementation savings remain unquantified. The Base therefore gives Athena no separate revenue or margin uplift. It does, however, keep R&D growing in line with the platform after FY2027, consistent with recent R&D elasticity and management's AI, Athena and platform priorities. Near-term R&D leverage would be a warning if it arrived before product consolidation, adoption and retention evidence: lower spending would improve reported margin, but could weaken the very product transition needed to make Zeta less media-dependent.

S&M Prior-year S&M + incremental S&M cost ratio × revenue increase 16.5% in FY2027, declining to 14.5% by FY2030 Tests the commercial cost of the next revenue dollar; existing-account expansion should be cheaper than repeatedly winning and implementing a new customer
R&D Direct growth in FY2027; thereafter revenue growth × elasticity 15% in FY2027; 1.0× elasticity from FY2028 Keeps product spending aligned with platform scale while Athena and the Marigold product set still require development and consolidation
G&A Direct growth in FY2027; thereafter the lower of a cap and revenue growth × elasticity 12% in FY2027; MIN(12%, 60% × revenue growth) thereafter Treats finance, legal, HR and central infrastructure as partly scalable, without letting election-supported revenue create a permanent overhead step-up

These choices form one coherent operating bet. Zeta does not need a richer mix or lower R&D to expand EBIT; it needs more revenue to pass through an already-built commercial and corporate platform. S&M uses an incremental formula because the question is how much extra selling cost accompanies extra revenue, not what arbitrary percentage of total revenue S&M should reach. The gradual improvement assumes customer expansion and sales integration help, but stops well short of immediately returning to the unusually low FY2024–FY2025 incremental cost ratios.

G&A is the less visible—and more demanding—part of the leverage case. One finance, legal or HR platform can support a larger company, but Marigold also added international entities, compliance work, product support and integration tasks. The 60% elasticity is therefore a middle course: it allows overhead to grow meaningfully, while assuming scale absorbs part of that growth. If those integration costs remain embedded rather than temporary, the Base will miss even if revenue and gross profit are correct.

Growth still requires cash investment

The model keeps equipment/data and software investment separate. FY2026 annualises H1 cash spending because management has not supplied a different H2 plan. FY2027 then grows equipment/data by 8% and software by 10%. From FY2028, the two categories respond to revenue growth at 40% and 50% elasticities, subject to the same 8% and 10% caps. Software receives the higher response because the product roadmap should move more with platform demand; equipment and data are steadier capacity and replacement programmes.

The important check is that FCFF does not appear merely because old assets run off. Cash investment continues to rise. D&A is higher at first because LiveIntent and Marigold purchase-accounting amortisation still passes through the income statement, then declines as those acquired assets are amortised. New equipment, software and data vintages continue to generate recurring D&A. The full cash-investment and vintage schedules stay in the model because they are audit support rather than a separate investment conclusion.

Zeta's customer concentration, agency relationships and sequential-liability clauses make receivables part of revenue quality rather than a mechanical cash-flow adjustment. The Base therefore forecasts each operating balance-sheet item and calculates investment as ending working capital less opening working capital. A move back toward 90 DSO could indicate collection, agency or campaign-quality problems and would materially reduce FCFF even if reported revenue remained intact.

The decline in SBC/revenue is an important part of per-share value, but the model does not obtain it by applying a revenue target. FY2026 starts with $105.1m of reported H1 expense and the filed $95.0m remaining schedule, plus a 3% allowance calibrated to five historical schedule updates. FY2027 combines the filed $116.9m existing-award schedule with $45.0m for later net award activity, close to the five-year historical median. From FY2028, SBC remains about 15.5% of S&M, R&D and G&A, Zeta's employee-intensive expense base. It therefore continues to rise in dollars as the organisation grows.

The primary FCFF valuation retains SBC inside operating expense. This avoids treating employee equity as free simply because it is non-cash in the current period. The model uses 257.4m valuation-date common-equity claims: 251.014m legal shares, 2.135m net dilution from vested exercisable options, 4.076m earned but unissued market-condition PSU shares and 0.189m earned but unissued legacy PSU shares. Unvested and unearned awards are not added wholesale because their future compensation cost remains in FCFF. At June 2026, the $381.4m unrecognised SBC balance and approximately 15.7m shares issued to LiveIntent and Marigold sellers remain the practical warning: enterprise value must grow faster than both employee and acquisition-related claims. These disclosures are in the Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q.

The 11.0% WACC is rounded from a market-input build shown in the model. The main judgement is the 1.50 equity beta: it sits above the relevant-sector range because Zeta has a shorter listed history, acquisition risk and unusually volatile shares, but below its noisy three-year observed estimate. The terminal assumptions test, rather than repair, the operating forecast. A 25% economic ROIC implies the reinvestment needed to support 3% perpetual growth; it does not overwrite FCFF. The Base's discounted terminal value is $3.70bn, or about 47% of its $7.83bn DCF enterprise value. The result is therefore sensitive to long-duration execution, but less dominated by terminal value than a short five-year DCF would be.

Sensitivity: valuation mechanics, operating execution and cash collection

The sensitivities answer three different questions and are therefore kept separate. The full WACC/perpetual-growth grid remains in the model; at the Base perpetual-growth rate, a 50bp WACC change moves value by roughly $2.4–$2.7 per share. That is duration and pricing risk. The operating grid below instead asks whether faster revenue growth creates margin, and the earlier DSO table isolates cash collection. Neither is an alternative scenario with its own probability.

The operating grid exposes the central interaction. A four-point revenue-growth shortfall can be largely offset if incremental S&M cost improves by four points; equally, four points of extra growth create almost no FY2030 margin benefit if commercial cost deteriorates by the same amount. Revenue beats are therefore not sufficient evidence. They matter only if incremental gross profit and sales efficiency convert them into EBIT. The scenario spread comes mainly from the duration and conversion of growth, not from changing WACC. Risks to this analysis

Several important disclosures remain incomplete. Zeta does not report post-close standalone gross profit, NOPAT or FCFF for LiveIntent or Marigold. Political-candidate revenue and broader political-and-advocacy revenue use different definitions, preventing a precise political-neutral historical series. Customer KPIs contain threshold migration and scope changes. Marigold carve-out costs include shared-service allocations and cannot be transferred directly into Zeta's post-close structure. Finally, contingent-consideration liability remeasurement and other discrete items make near-term GAAP EPS less useful than the operating and cash diagnostics discussed above.

The analysis responds to these gaps through explicit analyst assumptions, scenario ranges and monitoring triggers. Missing disclosure is not treated as a reason to leave a necessary forecast entry blank, but neither is an unsupported estimate presented as a filed fact.
