Enterprise marketing data and activation platforms

A growing but concentrated industry in which software, identity data and media execution carry very different margins—and reported revenue can obscure the profit pool.

Industry view

The best independent vendors will own consented data, interoperable identity and embedded workflows. The likely losers are opaque data resellers and media intermediaries valued as software. For investors, gross profit and SBC-adjusted FCF per share are more comparable than revenue.

Definition and value chain

One customer workflow, five economic layers

The relevant market is narrower than digital advertising and broader than marketing automation. It begins with customer-data ingestion and identity resolution, passes through decisioning and journey orchestration, and ends in owned-channel messaging or paid-media activation and measurement.

Data

First-party, partner and public signals; consent and deletion controls.

Identity

Link profiles, devices and households; govern addressability.

Decisioning

Predict intent, segment audiences and select next actions.

Activation

Email, SMS, web, CTV, social and programmatic media.

Measurement

Attribution, incrementality and feedback into the next campaign.

Zeta spans all five layers through its data cloud, CDP+, email service provider, DSP and service capability. That breadth can win a larger share of enterprise budgets, but it also mixes software subscriptions with utilisation, services and third-party media.

Revenue and profit pools

Scarce attention captures the largest pool; software quality depends on mix

U.S. internet advertising reached $294.6bn in 2025, up 13.9%, while programmatic revenue increased 20.5%. The market is not fragmented: the ten largest companies captured 84.1% of revenue, up from 80.8% in 2024. IAB/PwC, published 16 April 2026.

Walled gardens and publishers control scarce consumer attention. Independent vendors earn attractive margins where they own software workflow, identity or measurement; margins fall when they act as principal for media inventory, pay messaging fees or supply labour-intensive implementation.

Zeta's lower gross margin does not prove an inferior product. It proves that a dollar of its revenue is economically different. The most useful bridge is therefore organic revenue → media and delivery cost → organic gross profit → normalised operating profit → SBC-adjusted FCF per share.

Competition and barriers

Integration is valuable; “AI” alone is not a moat

Zeta competes with enterprise suites such as Adobe and Salesforce, engagement specialists such as Braze and Klaviyo, independent activation through The Trade Desk, identity platforms such as LiveRamp, agencies and customer-built cloud stacks. No single market-share figure is decision-useful because the products and revenue presentation differ.

Data provenance

Scale is an asset only when consent, use rights and deletion can be demonstrated.

Identity resolution

Deterministic links and match rates improve activation and measurement.

Integration depth

Production connectors and embedded workflows raise switching cost.

Governance

Security, privacy and auditability lengthen sales cycles but favour scaled vendors.

AI can reduce the cost of campaign creation and analysis, but it may commoditise surface-level features. Durable value should accrue to platforms combining governed data, permissioned activation and closed-loop outcomes—not merely a natural-language interface.

Regulation and platform policy

The cookie cliff receded; the consent burden did not

Google said in April 2025 that Chrome would retain its existing third-party-cookie choice approach rather than introduce a new standalone prompt. Google Privacy Sandbox. This removed an immediate deprecation cliff but did not reverse mobile-ID restrictions, consent requirements or regulator scrutiny.

California's DROP mechanism opened to consumers on 1 January 2026, and registered data brokers had to begin processing deletion requests on 1 August. California Privacy Protection Agency. In the UK, the ICO's April 2026 guidance covers cookies, pixels, fingerprinting and similar technologies under PECR and, where relevant, UK GDPR. ICO, 29 April 2026.

Compliance is both a cost and a barrier. Vendors able to propagate consent and deletion across derived identifiers and partners should consolidate share; vendors dependent on opaque third-party data face attrition and enforcement risk.

12–24 month outlook

Five changes to watch

01 · Quality

Gross profit replaces revenue as the key growth KPI

Post-acquisition comparisons will make direct mix, media cost and incremental margin harder to ignore.

02 · Data deletion

Policy becomes an operating test

California DROP processing can expose data attrition, match-rate changes and the true cost of compliance.

03 · AI adoption

Commercial evidence replaces feature claims

Winning platforms will show paying deployments, faster implementation, retention or lower service cost.

04 · Consolidation

Interoperable data remains strategically scarce

Oracle exited advertising in 2024; Publicis agreed to acquire LiveRamp in May 2026 for $2.167bn enterprise value.

05 · Stack rationalisation

Integrated platforms can displace point tools

Winners must simplify the customer stack without trapping data or diluting software economics with low-margin pass-through.

Investment read-through

What industry structure means for Zeta

The industry evidence supports above-market growth from enterprise share gains, high retention and account expansion. It does not support valuing every dollar of Zeta revenue like a pure subscription dollar. Q2 is the clearest test: ex-Marigold revenue grew 28%, but the most favourable ex-Marigold gross-profit calculation remains below 19.1%.

Bottom line

Zeta has a credible right to win because it combines data, identity, decisioning and activation. The same breadth creates opacity and cost. The investment case turns on whether above-market revenue becomes above-market gross profit and analyst-adjusted cash flow per share.
