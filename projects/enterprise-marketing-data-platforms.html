Enterprise Marketing Data and Activation: Why Revenue Growth Is Not Software Growth

Software, data, messaging and media can share one revenue line without sharing the same economics.

Research date: 9 September 2026 Industry framework By Jiefu Zhang

Enterprise marketing data and activation is better understood as an economic chain than as one software category. An enterprise collects customer data, resolves identities, decides what action to take, delivers a message or advertisement and measures the result. Software, data, messaging, media and labour can all sit inside the same contract—and sometimes the same reported revenue line—but they do not carry the same margin, bargaining power or cash requirements.

The industry is attractive because enterprises still need to acquire and retain customers across an increasingly fragmented set of channels. It is difficult because much of the scarce consumer attention, identity and advertising distribution remains controlled by large platforms. Independent vendors can create valuable coordination, workflow and measurement layers around those platforms, but reported revenue does not reveal how much of the customer budget they actually retain.

This leads to the governing investor question:

Who controls the data, workflow, distribution and measurement—and how much gross profit and cash remain after every other participant is paid?

Industry view in one page

Five conclusions organise the analysis:

  1. Demand is expanding, but not every vendor receives the same economics. U.S. internet-advertising revenue grew 13.9% in 2025, while 2026 buyer surveys point to faster growth in social, CTV and commerce media. Marketing budgets, however, remain broadly flat as a percentage of customer revenue. Vendors must win wallet share or displace other spending rather than rely on an unconstrained budget pool.
  2. Revenue is not a common unit across the stack. A subscription dollar, a messaging dollar, a professional-services dollar and a media dollar reported gross can represent very different retained economics. Gross profit is a better first comparison; incremental commercial cost and working capital complete the test.
  3. The largest profit pools follow control points. Consumer platforms and publishers control attention and inventory; enterprise suites and engagement platforms can control workflow; identity and data-collaboration vendors can control permissioned interoperability; agencies can control access to the budget. An independent platform usually earns a coordination rent—not the full distribution rent.
  4. Privacy and AI strengthen systems, not isolated features. Privacy reduces unrestricted identity signals but increases the value of consent provenance, first-party data and interoperable measurement. AI lowers the cost of producing features and campaigns, increasing the relative value of governed data, execution rights and credible outcome measurement.
  5. Wallet expansion can improve customer embeddedness while lowering gross margin. This is not automatically good or bad. It creates value only when lower incremental selling cost and stronger retention outweigh the delivery cost and working-capital burden of the added activity.

1. Start with the value chain, not the category label

The market begins when an enterprise collects customer information and ends when a marketing action produces a measurable outcome. Five layers connect those points:

Layer Customer task Common charging model Scarce input or constraint
Customer data and identity Ingest, govern and connect records across systems Subscription, usage and data-access fees Permission, match quality and continuous identity refresh
Intelligence and decisioning Predict intent, form audiences and select the next action Subscription and utilisation Governed data, compute and measurable model performance
Journey orchestration and messaging Coordinate email, SMS, mobile, web and loyalty activity Subscription plus message or profile volume Deliverability, implementation and support
Paid-media activation Buy or optimise display, video, CTV, social and commerce inventory Platform fee, percentage of spend or media spread Access to demand, inventory and platform APIs
Measurement and services Attribute outcomes, analyse performance and implement workflows Fees, retainers and professional services Data access, causal credibility and labour capacity

The same enterprise may buy these layers from a broad suite, several specialists, an agency and its own cloud-data team. Adobe and Salesforce compete through installed enterprise suites; Braze and Klaviyo focus more heavily on customer engagement; LiveRamp focuses on identity and data collaboration; The Trade Desk operates an independent demand-side platform. Agencies and customer-built data stacks can assemble or replace parts of the chain.

This is why a single market-size or market-share statistic is usually misleading. A marketing-automation estimate excludes paid activation and identity. A digital-advertising estimate includes publishers and consumer platforms that are suppliers or channels rather than direct software competitors. The defensible approach is to compare participants by the customer task they perform, the cost they carry and the control point they own.

2. One campaign creates different financial objects

A campaign normally follows the same operational sequence:

  1. The enterprise supplies customer, transaction and consent data.
  2. Identity technology connects records to people, households or devices.
  3. Decisioning software selects an audience, offer and channel.
  4. The campaign is delivered through an owned channel or paid inventory.
  5. Outcome data return to the platform and influence the next decision.

The accounting diverges at step four. In owned channels such as email or in-app messaging, the vendor normally retains most of the fee after cloud, traffic and delivery costs. In paid media, cash may pass from the advertiser through a platform to publishers, exchanges and data or measurement providers.

If the platform controls the promised service and acts as principal, third-party media spend may be recorded inside both revenue and cost of revenue. If it acts as agent, only the retained fee may be revenue. The same underlying campaign can therefore generate very different reported revenue and gross-margin percentages without changing the dollar gross profit retained by the intermediary.

Three quantities must remain separate:

  • customer marketing spend or billings: the amount committed to the activity;
  • platform revenue: the amount recognised under the vendor's contract and principal-versus-agent judgement; and
  • retained gross profit: platform revenue after inventory, data, messaging, cloud and other direct delivery costs.

This is not an accounting footnote. It determines whether EV/revenue, reported growth and NRR are economically comparable across companies. It also affects cash conversion: a vendor that invoices the customer before paying media suppliers may temporarily benefit from float, while a vendor that funds inventory or offers long payment terms can show strong accounting growth and deteriorating working capital.

3. Profit pools follow control, not the word “platform”

U.S. internet-advertising revenue reached $294.6bn in 2025, up 13.9%, and programmatic revenue reached $162.4bn, up 20.5%. Yet the ten largest companies captured 84.1% of industry revenue, up from 80.8% in 2024. IAB/PwC Internet Advertising Revenue Report, FY2025.

The concentration is not merely a survey observation. In April 2025, the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Virginia held that Google had monopolised open-web publisher ad-server and ad-exchange markets. The ruling provides direct evidence that control of distribution and auction infrastructure can entrench bargaining power even when competing software exists. U.S. Department of Justice, 17 April 2025.

The industry therefore contains several different rents:

Participant Main control point Source of economics Principal weakness
Consumer platforms and large publishers Audience, identity and inventory Distribution rent, auction economics and proprietary measurement Regulation, traffic shifts and advertiser concentration
Enterprise suites and engagement platforms Daily workflow, customer records and campaign logic Subscription, usage and switching friction Bundling pressure, implementation cost and feature commoditisation
Identity and data-collaboration vendors Permissioned matching across systems Interoperability, data governance and measurement Signal loss, customer-owned identity and platform restrictions
Independent activation platforms Cross-channel buying and optimisation Fee or spread on media activity Dependence on third-party inventory, APIs and measurement
Agencies and systems integrators Budget access, implementation and strategic advice Service fees and influence over vendor selection Labour intensity and client insourcing

Independent vendors are most valuable when they reduce the customer's coordination cost across channels without becoming a low-margin pass-through layer. Integration creates an economic advantage only if the savings from shared data, workflow, sales and measurement exceed the additional media, messaging, support and working-capital cost. Product breadth alone does not establish that outcome.

Public companies isolate parts of the value chain. They are useful as activity references, not as mechanically interchangeable peers.

The comparison supports three conclusions. First, “software” is not costless: message delivery, cloud infrastructure and onboarding can reduce gross margin even in subscription-led models. Second, media-heavy vendors can create real value at a lower percentage margin if the retained gross-profit dollars scale efficiently. Third, revenue multiples become least informative precisely when a company combines these activities in one segment.

5. The growth-quality test: wallet share can rise while margin falls

Customer expansion metrics reveal demand continuity, but not the source or economics of the expansion.

  • NRR measures revenue retained from a starting customer cohort after expansion, contraction and churn. It can rise through price, software modules, message volume, services or media spend.
  • Customer count depends on the disclosed definition. A threshold cohort can gain members when existing customers cross the threshold, not only when new organisations are won.
  • ARPU measures spending intensity within a reported population. It can rise because wallet share improves, media billings expand or small customers leave.

The more useful diagnostic compares delivery economics with marginal commercial effort:

Equation 1 Incremental commercial-contribution proxy (Change in gross profit − change in S&M) ÷ change in revenue = Incremental gross margin − incremental S&M cost ratio

The proxy is not a full contribution margin; it is a directional test of how much of each additional revenue dollar remains after direct delivery cost and incremental S&M.

Observed path Likely mechanism Interpretation
Incremental GM falls; incremental S&M falls by more Existing customers add media or messaging through an already-won relationship Potentially constructive wallet expansion and deeper workflow adoption
Incremental GM falls; incremental S&M stays high or rises More delivery-intensive revenue still requires heavy selling, implementation or support Low-quality growth; revenue scale is not converting into economic scale
Incremental GM is stable or rises; incremental S&M falls Software/data cross-sell, pricing or improving commercial productivity Strongest operating path
Incremental GM is stable or rises; incremental S&M rises sharply New-logo investment or entry into a new market May be rational, but payback and retention must justify the acquisition cost

Working capital is the necessary overlay. Media and agency-heavy contracts can increase receivables, supplier balances and sequential-liability exposure. A constructive decline in incremental S&M is not enough if the business must finance a growing share of customer spend. The full sequence is therefore:

revenue growth → gross-profit conversion → incremental commercial cost → working-capital conversion → per-share cash value

This sequence also explains why a falling gross margin is not automatically evidence of deterioration. It can reflect deeper adoption of a lower-margin channel by an existing customer. The warning appears when incremental GM keeps declining without offsetting commercial efficiency, cash conversion or retention.

6. A moat is a reinforcing system, not a feature list

The most defensible industry position combines several assets:

  • Permissioned data and identity quality. Data are valuable only when the vendor can lawfully use, link, refresh and delete them. Raw record count without provenance is closer to a compliance liability than a moat.
  • Embedded workflow. Customer-specific data models, campaign rules, governance and reporting create switching friction when they operate reliably in production. Connector count alone is weak evidence.
  • Cross-channel execution. A vendor that can act across owned channels and paid inventory reduces coordination cost and observes more of the customer journey. Dependence on one walled garden weakens this advantage.
  • Credible measurement. The ability to demonstrate incremental outcomes protects budget and pricing better than attribution dashboards that merely assign credit. IAB's 2025 commerce-media guidance emphasises counterfactuals, bias control and separation of signal from noise. IAB and IAB Europe, 3 November 2025.
  • Enterprise trust and governance. Security, privacy controls and auditability raise the cost of entry and can accelerate subsequent procurement once a vendor is established.

Privacy-preserving collaboration strengthens this system. IAB Tech Lab reports that 64% of companies using privacy-preserving technologies were using data clean rooms, and it has finalised protocols for privacy-centric audience matching and attribution. This shows that interoperability is becoming infrastructure rather than an experimental feature. It also means that no vendor can treat a proprietary clean-room interface as a permanent moat by itself. IAB Tech Lab Data Clean Rooms guidance.

Several popular moat claims are weaker:

  • generic AI features built on widely available models;
  • large quantities of third-party data with unclear or non-exclusive rights;
  • high media revenue that mainly passes supplier cost through the income statement;
  • high revenue-based NRR without gross-profit retention; and
  • product breadth assembled through acquisitions before data, workflow, support and sales teams are integrated.

The moat test is observable: retention through product changes, expansion in gross profit, lower marginal commercial cost, defensible pricing and cash conversion. A feature list describes capability; these outcomes demonstrate advantage.

7. Four structural shifts are redistributing value

7.1 Privacy creates a permissioned-identity premium—and a smaller signal pool

Google's April 2025 decision to retain Chrome's existing user-choice approach removed the immediate assumption of complete third-party-cookie deprecation, but it did not reverse the longer-term restriction of cross-site identity. Google Privacy Sandbox update, 22 April 2025. Apple's AppTrackingTransparency framework already requires user permission before an app can track activity across other companies' apps and websites or access the advertising identifier. Apple Developer privacy guidance.

Regulation moves in the same direction. California's Delete Request and Opt-Out Platform began processing data-broker requests in August 2026, while the UK's April 2026 guidance reinforced consent requirements for cookies, pixels, fingerprinting and similar technologies. California Privacy Protection Agency; UK ICO, 29 April 2026.

The economic effect is two-sided. A smaller pool of usable external signals can reduce reach, match rates and measurement quality. At the same time, enterprises place greater value on authenticated first-party data, consent lineage, deletion controls and secure collaboration. Privacy therefore does not simply help or hurt the sector; it shifts value from raw data volume toward permissioned identity and governance.

7.2 AI cheapens the interface but raises the value of data and execution

Gartner's 2026 survey found that CMOs allocated 15.3% of marketing budgets to AI, while only 30% reported mature or fully developed readiness. Overall marketing budgets were only 7.8% of company revenue, broadly unchanged from 7.7% in 2025. Gartner 2026 CMO Spend Survey. IAB's 2026 buyer study likewise found that five of the top six buyer priorities involved AI and that two-thirds focused on agentic AI for campaign execution. IAB 2026 Outlook Study.

The implication is budget reallocation, not an automatic expansion of the marketing wallet. Generic campaign creation, audience suggestions and workflow interfaces should become cheaper and easier to reproduce. Value should migrate toward the assets an agent still needs but cannot manufacture: lawful customer data, reliable identity, permission to execute, inventory access and outcome feedback.

AI can also increase activity faster than it reduces cost. Cheaper creative and automated campaign management may produce more audience variants, messages and media transactions. That can raise usage revenue while also raising cloud, messaging and inventory cost. The operating test is therefore paid adoption, shorter implementation, lower support burden, stronger incremental commercial contribution and better retention—not the number of AI features launched.

7.3 Channel growth increases the value of coordination—and the cost of fragmentation

IAB's 2026 outlook projects the fastest U.S. ad-spend growth in social media (14.6%), CTV (13.8%) and commerce media (12.1%). Total growth is projected at 9.5%, but falls to 7.1%–7.8% after excluding major cyclical events. IAB 2026 Outlook Study.

These channels are attractive because they combine rich audience or transaction data with measurable activity. They are difficult because each platform can retain its own identity, buying interface and attribution rules. The fragmentation gives integrated marketing platforms a coordination opportunity, but it also keeps the underlying channel owners in a strong bargaining position.

Measurement is the contested control point. Retail and commerce media can connect advertising exposure with purchase data, but attribution is not the same as incrementality. The industry's move toward common measurement standards reflects both the value of closed-loop data and the lack of comparability across networks. Vendors that can reconcile outcomes across channels should gain relevance; vendors that merely aggregate platform-reported attribution remain vulnerable.

7.4 Consolidation buys capability faster than it proves integration

Customers want fewer vendors, faster implementation and a consistent data layer. Suppliers respond through bundling and M&A. Oracle ended its advertising products in September 2024, while Publicis agreed in May 2026 to acquire LiveRamp for a stated enterprise value of $2.167bn. Oracle Advertising end-of-life FAQ; Publicis transaction announcement.

The two events point in the same direction: strategically useful data and interoperability can be absorbed into broader distribution systems, while subscale or non-core tools can disappear. An acquisition can buy an installed customer base, missing channel or data relationship faster than internal development. It does not automatically integrate product architecture, consent rights, customer support or sales incentives.

The correct M&A test is post-close gross profit, retention, cash flow and per-share return after integration cost and consideration—not the increase in revenue or adjusted EBITDA at closing.

8. A practical investor scorecard

The most useful metrics follow the economic chain rather than the vendor's product categories:

Question Best available evidence Positive signal Warning signal
Is demand durable? Cohort retention, customer additions, product adoption and usage Expansion is broad across customers and channels Growth depends on one customer, agency, event or acquisition
Is the platform retaining economics? Gross-profit growth and incremental GM Gross profit grows with activity Revenue outruns gross profit persistently
Is integration reducing commercial cost? Incremental S&M and implementation burden Existing-customer expansion requires less marginal selling effort S&M remains high despite cross-sell claims
Does data create bargaining power? Match quality, permissioned data coverage, retention and measured outcomes Customers renew because performance is observable Record-count claims without outcome or provenance evidence
Is growth converting into cash? DSO, supplier terms, working capital and operating investment Cash conversion holds as media volume grows Receivables or funding needs rise faster than gross profit
Does AI improve economics? Paid adoption, implementation time, support capacity and gross profit Automation improves throughput or customer outcomes AI spend rises without revenue, retention or cost evidence
Does consolidation create value? Post-close retention, cross-sell, cost removal and per-share cash flow Shared platform and distribution improve returns Revenue grows while dilution, integration cost and technical debt accumulate

Over the next 12–24 months, the decisive industry evidence will be:

  1. whether paid-media and commerce growth remains above the underlying 7%–8% event-adjusted buyer outlook;
  2. whether agentic marketing tools become paid workflow products or remain bundled features;
  3. whether privacy enforcement measurably reduces identity coverage or raises data and compliance cost;
  4. whether clean-room and measurement standards improve interoperability or further empower the largest platforms;
  5. whether vendors disclose gross-profit retention, media spend, take rate or product-level economics; and
  6. whether suite consolidation produces observable customer and cash-flow benefits rather than only larger reported revenue.

Industry conclusion

Enterprise marketing data and activation is a durable demand category but an uneven profit pool. Enterprises need governed customer data, coordinated execution and credible measurement; that need becomes more important as channels fragment and AI increases campaign complexity. The scarce assets are not generic software features. They are lawful identity, embedded workflow, distribution access and trusted outcome data.

The strongest independent vendors will use those assets to retain more gross profit while lowering the marginal cost of expanding customer relationships. The weaker ones will report the customer's media or messaging activity as growth without acquiring comparable bargaining power or cash economics. For investors, the distinction becomes visible only by following the chain from revenue to gross profit, incremental commercial cost, working capital and per-share cash value.
