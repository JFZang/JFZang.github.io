Industry view

1. Executive summary

Current industry assessment

Baijiu is not merely an alcoholic beverage. Its economics combine consumption, a social signal in banquets, a readily legible gift price and—for a narrow set of bottles—collectable value. Brand recognition and occasion utility permit leading products to sell far above production cost: leading producers report gross margins of 80–90%, while Moutai's FY2024 net margin attributable to parent shareholders exceeded 50%. The same demand structure makes high-priced baijiu sensitive to business confidence and public-sector conduct rules. Among leading producers, payment before delivery also places inventory and credit exposure downstream, so manufacturer earnings depend partly on wholesale prices and distributor balance-sheet capacity.

From 2021 to 2024, the published comparable-basis series showed output declining each year while revenue and total profit continued to rise. That offset failed in 2025: above-threshold producers reported output down 12.1%, revenue down 7.5% and total profit down 13.3%. The decline continued across the listed-company sample in the first half of 2026 (1H26). Section 3 sets out the statistical discontinuities in the reported industry series; Sections 9 and 10 examine the company-level cycle break.

Current evidence points to continued aggregate-volume contraction, while profit share can still concentrate among producers that preserve consumer pull and channel participation. This is an industry direction rather than a quantified sector forecast. Historical gross margin does not identify the likely beneficiaries. The relevant tests are a positive spread between repeatable wholesale price and effective distributor acquisition cost; falling inventory months without forced discounting; producer cash and recognised revenue that can be reconciled with later sell-through rather than new distributor credit; and an adequate post-maturation return on new capacity. Failure affects, respectively, producer net revenue per bottle, sustainable shipments, earnings quality and the duration of excess returns used in subsequent company analysis.

Key analytical conclusions

  1. Net-income concentration is much higher than revenue concentration within the observable listed-company set. The six largest listed baijiu producers (by FY2024 revenue)—Moutai, Wuliangye, Fenjiu, Luzhou Laojiao, Yanghe and Gujinggong—generated 86.5% of the 20 listed baijiu companies' adjusted operating-revenue proxy and 93.6% of their attributable net income in 2024. Their indicative share of the broader above-threshold producer revenue pool was 47.7%.
  2. Manufacturers have low visible leverage but the channel carries hidden burden. Prepayment and distributor borrowing transfer working-capital risk away from the producer. A net-cash manufacturer can still be exposed to substantial financial stress embedded in its distribution channel.
  3. Policy-led removal of demand for expensive baijiu does not guarantee substitution into lower-priced baijiu. Demand may trade down, disappear, shift into private gifting, or move to other drinks.
  4. Production scarcity should be distinguished from commercial scarcity. Old fermentation pits, local microbial ecology and aged base-liquor inventories constrain the supply of high-quality output. Premium pricing, however, also depends on brand recognition, controlled product release and channel price discipline.
  5. Shipment is no longer the most useful leading indicator. Wholesale price relative to effective distributor acquisition cost, channel inventory, repeat remittance and verified bottle-opening data—usually generated when a bottle code is scanned at opening—are more informative at this stage of the cycle. Producer cash must also be reconciled with contract liabilities and receivables because it can arrive before or after revenue recognition.

Sources of value

2. Sources of economic differentiation

2.1 Occasion-based demand

At some Chinese business banquets, shared drinking serves as a costly signal of respect, commitment and trustworthiness. Ethnographic observation and a discrete-choice experiment conducted among experienced Chinese business participants found support for the idea that heavy drinking during banquets can function as a trust signal. The authors caution that the sample is not representative of all regions or industries (Lin et al., 2025).

High-end baijiu fits these occasions because its price is widely recognised and the brand can signal the host's assessment of the guest's status. In gifting, a nationally recognised bottle reduces information asymmetry because the recipient can infer its approximate value without expert tasting knowledge. The economic consequence is a willingness to pay based partly on social recognition rather than sensory quality alone; business confidence and conduct rules therefore affect both volume and price mix.

Collectable value is narrower. A study of 20,035 auction lots from 2010–2023 found that brand, vintage and packaging materially affected prices, but also reported high volatility and low liquidity. Secondary-market value can reinforce scarcity for selected Moutai and vintage bottles; it is not evidence that ordinary baijiu inventory is an investment asset or that auction demand supports category-wide sell-through (Li, Masset and Weisskopf, 2026).

2.2 Regional density and national recognition

Consumption remains geographically embedded. Regional banquet customs, flavour preferences, family networks and local business relationships can support very high provincial shares for local champions. National expansion requires a brand to transcend regional identity while funding advertising, distributor recruitment and price management.

This supports two broad routes to attractive returns. National brands can monetise broad gifting and business recognition; regional leaders can combine outlet density with strong banquet penetration. National expansion creates value only when newly appointed distributors generate repeat orders at viable transaction prices.

2.3 Location-based production ecology

Baijiu uses open, multi-microbial fermentation. Raw materials, qu—the microbial starter used to convert grain starch and initiate fermentation—pit mud, climate and the production environment can influence microbial communities and flavour. Official geographical-indication materials for Moutai describe the local climate, soil and microbial environment, while peer-reviewed work supports an environmental effect on fermentation ecology (Standardisation Administration of China, 2025; Zhang et al., 2024).

Environmental influence can differentiate production, but its economic value depends on whether the resulting product commands persistent consumer demand and a repeatable price premium. Ecology alone does not establish irreplicable quality or pricing power.

Profit-pool architecture

4. Industry structure and channel economics

4.1 Functional map

Grain / packaging / equipment
                    ↓
Producer: qu-making → fermentation → distillation → storage → blending → bottling → brand
                    │
                    ├── Distributor → sub-distributor → retail outlet → consumer
                    ├── Key account (KA): retail chain / e-commerce platform → consumer
                    └── Producer direct → app / own store / group sales → consumer

The terms distributor, sub-distributor and retailer describe commercial functions rather than mutually exclusive legal categories. A first-tier distributor obtains a regional or product allocation from the producer. Sub-distributors provide local coverage. Retailers sell to the final consumer. A key account (KA) is a large chain or strategically important retail customer managed directly or under dedicated commercial terms. Large supermarkets and digital platforms may perform both wholesale procurement and retailing.

Grain, bottles, caps, cartons and equipment are upstream purchases, while leading producers generally retain qu-making, fermentation, storage and blending. Input-cost inflation has the greatest earnings effect in mass-market products with low gross profit per case; in premium baijiu, brand- and channel-driven pricing is the larger profit-pool determinant. Supplier dependence and packaging quality still matter through continuity of production, product presentation and anti-counterfeit control.

In this report, remittance means cash paid by a distributor to the producer for an order or allocation. Channel support comprises producer-funded rebates, free goods, promotion, price protection and other spending used to maintain distribution or transaction prices. An earnings bridge deducts support recorded as selling expense, but does not deduct again any discount already netted from recognised revenue.

Price inversion exists when the repeatable wholesale price achievable by a first-tier distributor—through sales to sub-distributors or retail accounts—falls below its effective acquisition cost. A price below the recommended retail price alone does not establish inversion.

Producers can capture part of the channel margin through direct sales. In 2024, Moutai reported gross margins of 95.3% in direct sales and 89.4% in wholesale agency, while Wuliangye reported 87.0% and 78.8%, respectively. Product mix differs between routes, so these are not causal estimates of a channel shift. Direct sales also move customer acquisition, fulfilment and channel-conflict costs to the producer; the relevant company-analysis input is incremental operating profit, not the reported gross-margin difference (Kweichow Moutai, 2025; Wuliangye, 2025).

4.3 Producer control over channel architecture

The physical act of distillation is only one part of the producer's economic role. A scaled baijiu company determines annual allocations, product architecture, distributor admission, payment timing, rebate rules, geographic boundaries, digital tracing and, in some cases, the conditions under which a distributor can obtain bank financing. The producer therefore governs a commercial system rather than merely selling packaged liquid.

This control is contractual rather than merely rhetorical. Golden Seed Liquor's 2025 distributor contracts required reporting of inventory, product flows and traceability codes; allowed the producer to inspect stock and suspend supply; and subjected out-of-territory sales to investigation and penalties. Its marketing support also required approved plans and documentary evidence. The disclosure comes from a smaller regional producer, but establishes that inventory visibility, territorial control and promotional verification are operating mechanisms used beyond the national leaders (Golden Seed Liquor, 2026).

This channel-governance role matters because reported gross margin captures the producer's share of value but not whether distributors earn enough after inventory holding and operating costs to accept the next allocation. A higher invoice price or lower rebate can improve current producer margin while reducing subsequent distributor remittance.

To assess the quality and sustainability of producer revenue, the analysis should follow the product and cash cycle:

producer allocation and invoice price → effective distributor acquisition cost → wholesale and group-purchase price → inventory days → cash remittance for the next allocation.

Section 8 examines how inventory and distributor financing transmit this pressure through the cycle; Section 12 sets out the corresponding channel-clearance scenario.

4.4 Distributor economics

The difference between the wholesale price and the producer invoice price is only the headline spread. The reconciliation to distributor profit is:

net sales proceeds from bottles sold, after discounts
- cost of bottles sold, after earned and collectable rebates
- inventory write-downs and losses on mandatory bundles
- logistics and warehousing
- local selling expense
- credit impairment losses
= distributor operating profit
- financing costs
= profit before tax

When wholesale prices fall, selling crystallises an inventory loss, while delaying the sale may postpone that loss—subject to any inventory write-down—but extends the holding period and financing cost. Current wholesale prices must therefore be matched to the acquisition cost and age of the inventory actually sold; comparison with the current producer invoice price can misstate both the realised spread and the inventory loss.

For the producer, the useful signal is whether distributors remit cash and reorder without additional borrowing, rebates or price protection. If repeat orders require more support, current shipments are running ahead of what the channel can fund from normal sales.

4.5 Sub-distributors and retail outlets

Sub-distributors solve a coverage problem. First-tier distributors cannot economically serve every lower-tier local market or maintain direct relationships with each restaurant, tobacco-and-liquor shop and event organiser. Sub-distributors purchase smaller lots and place them with local outlets, converting the producer's regional allocation into local availability. They earn a narrower distribution spread and may receive short-term trade credit from the first-tier distributor.

ZJLD Group Inc. (ZJLD) provides a disclosed example of the resulting network depth. Its distribution partners were permitted to appoint sub-distributors, and the company estimated approximately 189,200 sub-distributors at end-2022. The number demonstrates reach rather than economic control: these outlets were not equivalent to producer-managed accounts, and their profitability and inventory were not consolidated by the producer (ZJLD, 2023).

Retail outlets perform different functions according to the consumption occasion. Tobacco-and-liquor shops are important for household purchases, gifts and small corporate orders because customers value immediate availability and confidence in product authenticity. Restaurants provide access to on-premise consumption but may charge listing fees or require promotional support. Event organisers aggregate large, event-specific orders, while e-commerce platforms provide national fulfilment and transparent price comparison.

Regional allocation also creates the risk of cross-regional diversion: a distributor resells product outside its authorised territory when prices are higher elsewhere or local inventory is excessive. Such sales can depress wholesale prices in the receiving market, undermine compliant distributors and weaken the producer's ability to maintain differentiated regional pricing. Producers often implement bottle-level codes and distributor-specific tracing to make diversion easier to detect, but cannot remove its economic incentive when allocated shipments exceed local sell-through.

4.6 Working-capital allocation across the chain

Payment before delivery separates cash collection from revenue recognition. An order alone does not create producer revenue. Cash received before the producer transfers control of the bottles is recorded as a contract liability; revenue is recognised only when control passes, usually on delivery or acceptance under the contract. A liquor merchant loan can supply that cash, but it does not change the recognition rule (Wuliangye, 2025; Wuliangye, 2026a).

Supply economics

5. Production economics and supply constraints

Tonnes of base liquor produced are not equivalent to premium-product supply because the liquor must pass two filters before sale: quality and time. Batch-level differences in microbial ecology and fermentation conditions affect both yield and quality, so only part of production may be suitable for a producer's premium products. Eligible base liquor must then be stored and blended across batches and production years before release. Total annual base-liquor production therefore cannot be translated directly into near-term premium-product capacity (Ye et al., 2021).

Capacity disclosure presents a separate measurement problem. Design capacity is the annual throughput for which a production system was engineered, not expected production or saleable output. Luzhou Laojiao reported both design and actual capacity of 170,000 tonnes in 2024, with another 80,000 tonnes in progress. At year-end it held 429,840 tonnes of semi-finished liquor, including base liquor, and 43,358 tonnes of finished liquor. Capacity, annual production and stored liquor are therefore different stocks and flows; none discloses the quantity that can be sold as a premium product without assumptions about grade, maturation and blending (Luzhou Laojiao, 2025).

Supply also need not originate entirely within the listed producer. ZJLD reported that a higher proportion of self-produced base liquor reduced its 2024 cost of sales as it replaced purchases from third-party distilleries. Own production, aged inventory and external sourcing can therefore change available volume and gross margin independently. A supply forecast must identify the source and quality of base liquor rather than infer branded-product economics from installed capacity alone (ZJLD, 2025).

Comparison with Western spirits clarifies where the delay occurs. Scotch whisky and Cognac concentrate much of it after distillation through cask maturation; baijiu supply is filtered first by fermentation-batch quality and then by grading, storage and blending. Vodka and gin generally have a shorter post-distillation cycle. Shede's disclosed base-liquor age requirements range from one to more than ten years across products, so even baijiu has no single maturation lag (Shede, 2025b; Scotch Whisky Association, n.d.; Bureau National Interprofessionnel du Cognac, n.d.).

Analytical implication. Product-level availability is derived from qualified current production, matured inventory and eligible external purchases, net of liquor retained for further ageing or blending. New capacity receives no forecast volume until commissioning, qualified yield, maturation and the intended destination product can be evidenced; actual shipments remain constrained by demand and release policy.

6.2 Policy-sensitive demand

Policy can affect baijiu demand through three distinct routes: direct removal from covered official occasions, spill-over into corporate entertainment norms, and substitution towards lower-priced products or private occasions. These effects should not be combined into an assumption that all spending displaced from expensive baijiu migrates to another baijiu price point.

The post-2012 anti-corruption episode provides directional precedent: a peer-reviewed policy review records rapid declines in luxury-alcohol sales and a change in the profile of frequent drinkers among officials after the measures. It establishes that official-conduct rules can affect high-end demand beyond the meals directly covered, but does not supply a stable elasticity for the 2025 rules (Hu et al., 2022).

The revised 2025 regulation states that work meals for covered Party and government bodies must not serve alcohol. It applies to specified public bodies and should not be described as a society-wide alcohol ban (State Council, 2025). The direct effect is the removal of alcohol from covered meals; the size of any spill-over or substitution effect remains an empirical question.

The first-half 2025 industry survey by KPMG and the China Alcoholic Drinks Association (CADA) indicates that weakness extended beyond official demand: 80.8% of responding distributors reported weaker banquet and gifting demand, while gatherings with friends and routine drinking also weakened; weddings and other events were relatively stable. The survey measures respondent sentiment rather than transaction-weighted national demand, but it argues against assuming one-for-one substitution from policy-sensitive demand into lower-priced baijiu (KPMG and CADA, 2025).

6.3 Demographics and broad consumption proxies

Demographics affect baijiu through participation and occasions rather than population alone. A large 2004–08 cohort found weekly drinking among 33% of men but only 2% of women, with male participation ranging from 7% to 51% across ten study areas. The observations are dated and not a current demand estimate, but establish that age, sex and region cannot be collapsed into a population coefficient (Millwood et al., 2013). Wedding demand is therefore tested against registrations and banquet size; business entertainment against corporate activity; and personal consumption against age-specific participation and frequency.

Broad consumption data provide a useful boundary but not a baijiu forecast. In 2025, national catering revenue grew 3.2% and retail sales by above-threshold tobacco-and-liquor retailers grew 2.7%, while above-threshold baijiu-producer revenue fell 7.5% on the official comparable basis. The first two series include activities and products far beyond baijiu; their divergence from producer revenue indicates that the relevant pressure was the mix of drinking occasions, price bands and channel inventory rather than a uniform contraction in all dining or alcohol-related spending. Population ageing likewise changes the potential consumer pool only through participation, health preferences and occasion frequency (NBS, 2026; Wuliangye, 2026a).

Faster sell-through does not necessarily make a price band more profitable: lower-priced products turn faster but may contribute less operating profit per case, while prestige products can tolerate slower turnover if their price spread remains positive. Producer models should therefore separate volume, producer net revenue per bottle, product cost and directly attributable channel-support expense rather than infer the profit pool from sell-through alone.

The 1H25 industry survey used different brackets and identified RMB100–300, RMB300–500 and below RMB100 as relatively better-moving, while respondents reported severe price inversion in RMB800–1,500, RMB500–800 and RMB300–500. These are multi-response observations rather than market-share estimates. The overlap at RMB300–500 indicates that comparatively better movement can coexist with weak economics when discounting drives volume; such sales do not support repeat orders unless the post-rebate distributor spread remains positive (KPMG and CADA, 2025).

7.2 Aroma type and supply response

Aroma classification matters because it changes fermentation, storage and the time between capacity spending and saleable output. It belongs in the supply forecast, not in an automatic valuation premium: price and margin still depend on the individual brand, product and channel.

No transparent, authoritative national dataset was identified that reconciles revenue share simultaneously by aroma, transaction-price band and producer. Internet estimates conflict materially. Aroma type is therefore treated as a production and competitive variable, not as a standalone basis for market-share or growth forecasts.

Channel balance sheet

8. Channel inventory and hidden leverage

Section 4 located most sales-related working capital at the distributor. Borrowing magnifies that exposure: a small fall in bottle value can cause a much larger loss of distributor equity and force stock to be sold.

8.2 Liquor merchant loans: structure and distinctive risks

A liquor merchant loan finances an authorised distributor's purchase from a core producer. The bank normally pays the producer directly rather than giving unrestricted cash to the distributor. Repayment is supported by the distributor, the underlying order and, depending on the facility, pledged inventory or delivery rights and controlled release of the goods.

The structure is supply-chain inventory finance, not a practice unique to baijiu. Automotive floorplan finance is a useful comparison: the bank validates a dealer order, pays the manufacturer and controls the vehicle or title document until repayment (China Construction Bank, n.d.). Baijiu is easier to divide and store, but bottle authenticity and liquidation value are less uniform. The key terms are therefore the advance rate—the loan as a percentage of eligible collateral value—custody and any producer guarantee or repurchase obligation.

8.3 Illustrative credit process

The process below synthesises disclosed bank structures. Product terms vary by bank and producer.

1. Producer confirms authorised distributor and purchase allocation
2. Bank verifies the distributor, purchase order, collateral and required equity contribution
3. Bank, distributor, producer and warehouse establish payment and collateral-control terms
4. Bank pays the producer directly; goods or delivery rights enter controlled custody
5. Distributor repays by batch as inventory is sold and collateral is released
6. Final repayment restores unrestricted title

The bank payment in step 4 provides producer cash. If control of the bottles has not yet passed, the producer records a contract liability rather than revenue.

Business-model quality

11. Moats and business-model quality

A baijiu moat is valuable only when it protects both consumer willingness to pay and distributors' ability to earn an adequate profit after inventory holding and operating costs. Historical gross margin, brand age and nominal capacity are not enough. The operating test is whether a producer can preserve repeatable transaction prices, positive distributor economics and cash generation during a down-cycle without replacing demand with credit, rebates or inventory accumulation.

The moat is therefore assessed as a linked system rather than a list of attributes:

consumer recognition and occasion relevance
→ repeatable transaction price
→ positive distributor return and voluntary replenishment
→ producer revenue and cash that reconcile with delivery and later sell-through
→ disciplined reinvestment and shareholder distribution.

A break in one link can be concealed temporarily by support or credit elsewhere in the system. Down-cycle evidence is therefore more informative than peak-cycle growth.

11.1 Brand recognition is not identical to consumer preference

Brand recognition has economic value when the buyer needs other people to recognise the bottle. This is particularly relevant in gifting and formal banquets. Consumer preference matters more in self-drinking, where the buyer bears the cost and consumes the product. A brand can therefore remain widely recognised while being consumed less often.

The distinction determines how long brand recognition can support excess returns. Recognition-led demand remains durable only if the occasions that display the bottle remain frequent; self-purchase preference becomes more important when business entertainment and formal gifting weaken. The company test is the divergence between brand awareness and observable behaviour: bottle openings, repeat purchase, banquet penetration, direct-to-consumer repurchase and the age profile of purchasers. Any long-run growth assumption requires evidence that younger cohorts are adopting, rather than merely ageing into, existing consumption patterns.

11.2 Price architecture as an intangible asset

A successful producer establishes clearly separated transaction-price anchors between the flagship, premium extensions, regional banquet products and mass-market range. The economic asset is not the number of products but the consumer's ability to infer status and value without comparing discounts. Overlapping products create internal substitution, increase retail selling effort and make discount arbitrage between products or regions harder to control.

In company-specific work, price architecture can be tested with a periodic map of list price, repeatable retail price, wholesale price and effective distributor acquisition cost for the principal products. At industry-primer level, persistent discounting or a narrowing gap between flagship and extension products is sufficient evidence of possible erosion; list-price increases receive no benefit until transaction prices and distributor spreads confirm them.

11.3 Production assets and the premium-liquor yield

Open fermentation creates real differences between batches, but a difficult-to-explain process is not itself a moat. Better microbial control, data tracking and quality control can reduce variation without reproducing an incumbent's aged pits, base-liquor stock or brand demand (Zhang et al., 2024). The useful evidence is stable premium-grade yield from established assets and adequate returns from new projects once their liquor is ready for sale.

11.4 Channel quality as part of the moat

Distribution breadth becomes an asset when local coverage lowers selling cost or improves product availability without sacrificing price discipline. Distributor additions should therefore be tested against exits, revenue per distributor and repeat remittance; first shipments to new or credit-funded accounts do not establish a durable network advantage.

11.5 Governance and capital allocation

Many leading baijiu companies are controlled by local state-owned entities, creating potential objectives beyond minority-shareholder return: tax contribution, employment, local investment, tourism and headline revenue growth. State ownership is not itself a valuation discount. The discount depends on observable cash deployment and whether operating targets conflict with channel economics.

The review should reconcile five years of CFO with dividends, baijiu capital expenditure, financial investments, acquisitions, related-party balances and other local projects. Capacity spending is evaluated against premium-liquor yield and credible demand for the intended product; unusually aggressive shipment targets are checked against wholesale prices and channel inventory. Distribution of surplus cash and restraint on low-return expansion support a longer duration of excess returns regardless of ownership. Persistent unrelated investment, cross-subsidisation or channel loading is reflected in normalised earnings and reinvestment assumptions rather than left as a qualitative governance warning.

13.2 Current tax framework

Baijiu is subject to consumption tax equal to 20% of the taxable sales value plus RMB0.5 per 500 grams or millilitres under the current statutory schedule (State Council, 2008). For liquor without an approved minimum taxable price, current reporting guidance compares the producer's related-party ex-factory price with 60% of the affiliated sales company's external price and uses the higher amount for filing. The rule limits profit shifting through an artificially low producer price; it does not impose a wholesale or retail tax (State Taxation Administration, 2026).

No enacted rule moving baijiu consumption tax comprehensively to wholesale or retail was identified. Such reform remains a scenario risk, not current law.

13.6 DCF assumptions and sensitivities

A DCF model adds value only if it separates physical volume, changes in realised price and product mix, margin and reinvestment. Entering a single revenue-growth assumption hides the principal debate.

Any company DCF should test a channel-reset period in which producer shipments grow more slowly than consumer sell-through. The downside case combines weaker volume with lower premium mix and higher channel-support expense; lowering volume alone understates operating leverage. An upside case requires evidence that wholesale prices recover without renewed distributor leverage.

Terminal growth must reflect declining industry volume. Positive nominal growth can still be justified through realised price, product mix and market-share gains, but it must remain consistent with long-run end demand and sustainable distributor returns. Terminal margin should not assume channel support falls below its cycle-average level until wholesale spreads and voluntary replenishment have normalised.

Net cash improves liquidity resilience and increases equity value through the enterprise-to-equity bridge, but it does not remove policy, brand or channel risk. The weighted average cost of capital should reflect both operating risk and the sustainable capital structure; excess cash is added separately rather than used to suppress the operating discount rate.

Closing assessment

14. Conclusion

Baijiu can still produce exceptional returns, but broad premiumisation and historical producer margin no longer describe the industry adequately. Contracting volume, weaker channel spreads and distributor leverage make sustainable earnings product- and route-specific. Scarce national flagships, regional franchises and sub-premium brands therefore require different shipment, support and reinvestment assumptions in subsequent company work.

The decisive analytical sequence is consumer sell-through, distributor economics and voluntary remittance, followed by producer revenue and cash-flow reconciliation. Evidence of industry normalisation requires these indicators to improve together; shipment growth financed by inventory accumulation or additional channel credit is not sufficient.

Important limitations

  • The broad listed-company cross-section remains an FY2024 snapshot; four representative companies are extended through FY2025.
  • Reported ROE is used because current disclosures do not support one consistent surplus-cash adjustment across the four companies. Project ROIC also requires project-level capital and earnings evidence that is not yet available.
  • National revenue shares by price band and aroma lack a transparent authoritative dataset.
  • Inventory held by distributors, retailers and collectors, and bottle-opening data, have no official continuous series; channel surveys are rarely sales weighted.
  • Academic research explains mechanisms but regional samples do not establish national demand shares.
  • Historical production levels are affected by changes in the population of enterprises meeting the official reporting threshold.
