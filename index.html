Working vocabulary

Glossary

This glossary records terms encountered throughout my research and reading. Each entry first establishes the relevant definition, before examining its economic interpretation and the limitations that should be considered when applying it in practice.

Browse glossary 8 terms
Earn-out Contingent acquisition payment
Simple explanation

An earn-out is the portion of acquisition consideration that becomes payable only if the acquired business satisfies specified post-transaction conditions. These conditions commonly refer to revenue, EBITDA, customer retention or product-development milestones measured over an agreed period.

Earn-out vs contingent consideration

An earn-out is generally a subset of contingent consideration, distinguished by its dependence on the subsequent performance of the acquired business. Contingent consideration is broader, as payment may instead be triggered by other events or contractual conditions that are not directly related to operating performance.

Why it matters

Earn-outs may reconcile different valuation expectations between the buyer and seller whilst reducing the buyer's initial capital at risk. Nevertheless, this arrangement can encourage short-term optimisation around the selected target and may give rise to disputes over its measurement. The accounting classification is also consequential: liability-classified consideration is generally remeasured after acquisition, whereas equity-classified consideration is generally not remeasured.

Interpretation

Interpretation therefore requires inspection of the performance metric, payment threshold, measurement period, maximum payout and accounting classification. In particular, a payment conditional upon the seller's continued employment may represent compensation rather than purchase consideration, thereby altering both the acquisition accounting and the subsequent earnings profile.

Analytical treatment

Fair-value remeasurement gains or losses on contingent consideration may be excluded from normalised operating earnings, since they do not directly represent the underlying operating performance achieved during the current period.

  • The contingent consideration itself should nevertheless not be disregarded.
  • Expected cash payments should remain within acquisition cost, liability or future cash-flow analysis, as appropriate.
  • Expected share issuance should be incorporated into the assessment of potential dilution.
Remeasurement volatility may be removed from operating profit, whilst the underlying economic cost should remain in enterprise-value, cash-flow and dilution analysis.
FIMA Repo Foreign and International Monetary Authorities Repo Facility
Simple explanation

The FIMA Repo Facility allows approved foreign central banks and other international monetary authorities to obtain temporary US dollar liquidity against Treasury securities held in custody at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York. It therefore provides an alternative to selling these securities outright in the market.

Transaction structure
Treasuries ⇄ US dollars Repurchase at maturity for cash received + repo interest
Why it matters

During periods of global dollar scarcity, foreign official institutions may need to raise dollars rapidly. By permitting them to finance rather than liquidate their Treasury holdings, the facility may reduce forced sales, the associated upward pressure on Treasury yields and the transmission of offshore funding stress into US financial conditions.

FIMA Repo vs dollar swap lines

FIMA Repo is secured by the participant's Treasury holdings and is available to approved FIMA account holders. Dollar liquidity swap lines, on the other hand, operate with a smaller group of central banks through exchanges of dollars for the counterparty central bank's currency. Hence, both instruments provide dollar liquidity backstops, but their collateral and institutional structures are distinct.

Interpretation

The facility is priced primarily as a backstop rather than a routine source of inexpensive funding. Consequently, a material increase in utilisation may indicate pressure in global dollar markets or an attempt to avoid disruptive Treasury sales. Nevertheless, utilisation alone is insufficient to establish that a participating institution faces a solvency problem.

Incremental economics Economics of additional growth
Simple explanation

Incremental economics describe the revenue, profit and cash return generated by an additional unit of growth relative to the further operating cost and invested capital required to produce it. The concept therefore focuses on changes at the margin rather than the average economics of the existing business.

Simplified formulas

Incremental margin is not self-defining, since its interpretation depends upon the profit measure selected for the numerator. Gross profit, operating profit and EBITDA consequently produce distinct measures and should not be used interchangeably.

Incremental gross margin = Δ Gross profit Δ Revenue
Incremental operating margin = Δ Operating profit Δ Revenue
Incremental ROIC = Δ NOPAT Δ Invested capital
Why it matters

Company-wide averages predominantly describe the accumulated economics of the existing business, whereas incremental measures reveal whether new growth is becoming more or less valuable. In particular, an improving incremental margin may indicate operating leverage and scalability, whilst a weak incremental return may suggest that growth requires a disproportionate amount of expenditure or capital.

Interpretation

The calculation should use comparable periods and distinguish organic movements from acquisitions, disposals, currency effects, cyclicality and unusual items. Moreover, a high incremental margin does not independently demonstrate value creation. The additional capital required, the persistence of the growth and the resulting incremental cash return remain essential to the assessment.

NOL Net Operating Loss
Simple explanation

A net operating loss arises when a company's allowable tax deductions exceed its taxable income for a given period. Subject to the applicable tax regime, the loss may be carried forward or backward to offset taxable income in other periods, thereby reducing future or past cash tax payments.

Simplified formula
Potential tax benefit ≈ Usable NOL × Applicable tax rate
Why it matters

NOLs may shelter future taxable profits and therefore possess economic value. However, the reported balance should not be interpreted as a readily realisable asset. Expiry dates, annual utilisation limits, changes in ownership, jurisdictional restrictions and the company's capacity to generate suitable taxable income may each reduce the amount ultimately utilised.

Interpretation

It is therefore necessary to distinguish the gross NOL balance from both the recognised deferred tax asset and the expected cash-tax benefit. Valuation should incorporate only the portion expected to be utilised within the relevant jurisdiction and permitted period, with the resulting tax savings discounted according to their expected timing.

NRR Net Revenue Retention
Simple explanation

Net revenue retention measures the recurring revenue retained from a fixed cohort of existing customers after incorporating expansion, contraction and churn, whilst excluding revenue contributed by newly acquired customers.

Formula
NRR = Starting recurring revenue + Expansion − Contraction − Churn Starting recurring revenue × 100%
Why SaaS uses it

NRR is most informative for SaaS and other subscription businesses, since their growth depends not only upon customer acquisition but also upon the retention and expansion of recurring revenue. By excluding new customers, the measure isolates whether the existing cohort grows through renewals, price increases and cross-selling. Its interpretation is less tractable for transactional or project-based businesses, where customer expenditure is irregular and non-recurring.

Interpretation

An NRR above 100% indicates that expansion among retained customers more than offsets contraction and churn. This may provide evidence of product value, customer stickiness and efficient growth. Nevertheless, comparisons require consistent cohort definitions, measurement periods and revenue perimeters, which may differ materially across companies.

PPA Purchase Price Allocation
Simple explanation

Purchase price allocation is the accounting process through which acquisition consideration is allocated to the identifiable assets acquired and liabilities assumed, generally at their acquisition-date fair values. Any residual consideration is ordinarily recognised as goodwill, whilst an excess of identifiable net assets may give rise to a bargain-purchase gain.

Simplified formula
Purchase price ≈ Fair value of identifiable net assets + Goodwill
Why it matters

PPA determines how an acquisition subsequently affects reported earnings. In particular, allocating greater value to finite-lived intangible assets, such as customer relationships, technology or brands, generates future amortisation expense. A greater allocation to goodwill avoids scheduled amortisation, but may instead increase the amount exposed to a subsequent impairment.

Interpretation

PPA should therefore be examined when reconciling reported and adjusted earnings, assessing whether an acquisition is genuinely accretive and evaluating the economics of management's capital allocation. Large goodwill balances, short assumed useful lives or the repeated exclusion of acquisition-related amortisation may materially alter the apparent profitability and return on invested capital of an acquisitive company.

RPO Remaining Performance Obligations
Simple explanation

Remaining performance obligations represent contracted revenue that a company expects to recognise in future periods for goods or services that have not yet been transferred to the customer. The measure generally combines deferred revenue with contracted consideration that has not yet been invoiced.

Simplified formula
RPO ≈ Deferred revenue + Contracted amounts not yet billed
RPO vs AR

Accounts receivable represents consideration that has generally been invoiced, with the related revenue already recognised but not yet collected in cash. RPO instead relates to contracted consideration for which revenue recognition remains incomplete because the associated performance obligation has not yet been satisfied.

Interpretation

RPO is particularly informative when assessing revenue visibility, backlog quality and changes in customer commitment within subscription or long-term contractual businesses. Current RPO, which is expected to be recognised within the following twelve months, is generally more relevant to near-term growth. Nevertheless, RPO should not be interpreted mechanically as a revenue forecast, since recognition timing, cancellation provisions, contract duration, acquisitions and billing structure may all affect the measure.

Valuation allowance Deferred tax asset valuation allowance
Simple explanation

A valuation allowance reduces a deferred tax asset to the amount expected to be realised. Under US GAAP, it is recognised when the weight of available evidence indicates that some or all of the deferred tax asset is more likely than not to remain unutilised.

US GAAP presentation
Net deferred tax asset = Gross deferred tax asset − Valuation allowance
Connection to NOLs

NOL carryforwards commonly give rise to deferred tax assets. Where insufficient evidence exists that the company will generate suitable taxable income within the permitted period, a valuation allowance reduces the recognised net asset. Notably, the underlying NOL continues to exist for tax purposes; the allowance instead reflects uncertainty concerning its accounting realisation.

Framework difference

IFRS contains no directly equivalent account or formal term corresponding to a valuation allowance. Under IAS 12, the same uncertainty is addressed at the recognition stage: a deferred tax asset arising from unused tax losses or credits is recognised only to the extent that sufficient future taxable profit or suitable reversing taxable temporary differences are considered probable. Consequently, the remaining potential benefit stays unrecognised rather than being recorded gross and offset by a separate allowance.

IFRS recognised DTA = Portion whose utilisation is probable

The unrecognised portion is reassessed at each reporting date and may subsequently be recognised if recovery becomes probable. Conversely, an existing deferred tax asset is reduced where sufficient future taxable profit is no longer expected. The economic outcome may therefore resemble that produced by a valuation allowance, although the terminology, recognition sequence and balance-sheet presentation remain different.

Interpretation

Establishing an allowance ordinarily increases tax expense, whereas releasing one produces a tax benefit; both effects are generally non-cash in the period of recognition. A release may provide evidence of improved expectations for future taxable income. Nevertheless, it should not automatically be classified as recurring operating profit or newly created economic value, since the underlying tax attribute existed prior to the accounting reassessment.
